Episode 13: Cytoplasmic Bleed
Cytokinesis is a violent act. Textbooks describe cell division with neat, clean diagrams—a perfect circle pinching into a perfect figure-eight, before peacefully snapping into two identical spheres.

But textbooks don't show the chaos of the cytoplasm. They don't show the fluid, the proteins, and the raw biological matter violently churning and boiling as it is aggressively squeezed into the ever-narrowing corridor of the cleavage furrow.

As the sun set over the Nevada desert, the sky above us finally broke.

The bruised magenta static that had dominated the heavens for three days suddenly began to curdle. Great swathes of the sky went pitch black, devoid of stars, moon, or light. But it wasn't the darkness of deep space. It felt close. Suffocating. It was as if a massive, opaque dome had been draped over our specific section of the desert.

"The nuclear envelopes are reforming," I said, staring through the thick glass of the Stryker’s viewing slit. The words fogged the cold glass. "The two new universes are building their own outer membranes to protect the genetic data. We are being walled off."

"So, we're in the clear?" Captain Miller asked from the driver's seat, his hands tight on the wheel. "If they're sealing up, doesn't that mean the division is over?"

"It means the exact opposite," Julian murmured from the corner, his eyes bloodshot and sunken. "The nuclei are sealed. But the cell body—the physical space we are driving through—is still connected. All the raw matter, the physics, the energy... it’s all being squeezed into a spatial corridor that is shrinking by the minute. It’s highly pressurized."

"Cytoplasmic mixing," Thorne stated, recalling my earlier warning. She was methodically loading armor-piercing rounds into her revolver. "You said the ghost data would become physical."

"Until now, the timeline errors were just visual static. Holograms of probability," I explained, turning away from the dark sky. "But the pressure in this furrow is immense. It forces the two realities to aggressively overlap. Things won't just flicker anymore, Dr. Thorne. They will fuse."

Stryker Two, driven by Silas, was rumbling fifty yards ahead of us, its heavy headlights cutting through the oppressive darkness.

Suddenly, the radio crackled. Silas’s gravelly voice filled the cabin.

"Thorne. Finch. You seeing this?"

"Seeing what, Silas?" Thorne grabbed the handset.

"The ground. The headlights are picking up something impossible."

Miller leaned forward, squinting through the armored windshield. Slowly, the monotonous grey of the cracked desert hardpan began to change. First, it was a subtle shift in texture. The dust turned into rich, dark loam. Then, patches of thick, emerald-green moss appeared, glowing unnaturally in the headlights.

Within half a mile, the desert simply ceased to exist.

We were driving into a dense, towering forest. Massive redwood trees, hundreds of feet tall, shot up from the ground, their trunks slick with mist. Giant ferns brushed against the steel hull of the Stryker. The air that leaked into the cabin was no longer dry and biting; it was thick, humid, and smelled of decaying wood and petrichor.

"A redwood rainforest," Chloe whispered, her eyes wide. "In the middle of the Black Rock Desert."

"It's not an illusion," Julian said, checking the analog barometer. "The humidity just spiked to ninety percent. The barometric pressure dropped. This is a physical biome."

"The other universe," I realized, a cold sweat breaking out on my neck. "In the multiverse branching, the parallel Earth formed differently. Maybe a shift in tectonic plates, or a different climate cycle. On their Earth, Nevada is a coastal rainforest."

"And the spatial pressure is squeezing their geography into ours," Thorne concluded, her eyes scanning the dense treeline. "Keep moving, Miller. Do not slow down. If the two biomes are overlapping, the terrain could shift back to solid rock at any second."

We pushed deeper into the impossible forest. The headlights cut through the thick fog, casting long, eerie shadows behind the massive tree trunks. It was beautiful, in a terrifying, surreal way.

Then, something moved in the trees.

"Movement, right side!" Miller shouted, instinctively tapping the brakes.

A massive shape stepped out of the ferns and into the glaring beam of the Stryker’s headlights.

It was a stag. An elk, massive and majestic.

But as it turned its head toward the idling armored vehicle, Chloe let out a muffled, horrified shriek.

It was not one stag. It was two.

The spatial overlap had forced two biological entities from two different universes into the exact same physical coordinates. But instead of annihilating each other, the immense pressure of the cytoplasm mix had fused them together.

The creature had two heads, branching off a grotesquely swollen, muscular neck. Its eyes were milky white and rolling in agony. It possessed six legs, two of which were fused together at the knee, snapping and reforming as it took a crippled, shuddering step forward. Its ribcage was a horrific jumble of exposed bone and phasing, glitching flesh.

It opened both of its jaws and let out a sound that would haunt my nightmares until the end of my life—a dual-toned, gurgling scream of an animal experiencing the absolute breakdown of its own biology.

"Chimera," I whispered, bile rising in my throat. "The matter is splicing. God, the pain it must be in..."

"Put it out of its misery," Thorne ordered coldly.

Before Miller could reach for his sidearm, the radio shrieked.

"Contact! Contact front!" Silas bellowed. "Brace for impact!"

Ahead of us, the brake lights of Stryker Two flared blindingly red. We heard the screech of heavy tires locking up on the wet forest floor, followed by a deafening crash of metal on metal.

"Silas!" Julian screamed, diving toward the windshield.

Miller slammed on our brakes. We skidded to a halt in the mud, mere inches from the rear bumper of Silas's Stryker.

"Report!" Thorne yelled into the radio. "Stryker Two, report! What did you hit?"

Static hissed for a torturous five seconds.

"We hit... a roadblock," Silas’s voice finally came through, breathless and shaken. "You need to see this, Thorne. But keep your weapons drawn."

Thorne kicked open the heavy rear doors. We poured out into the humid, mist-filled forest, our boots sinking into the wet mud. The air smelled of pine needles and ruptured diesel lines.

We ran up to the front of Silas’s vehicle. The heavy steel ram-bumper of Stryker Two had plowed into something massive, crushing the front end of an opposing vehicle.

It wasn't a fallen redwood tree. It wasn't a civilian car.

It was another Stryker M1126 Infantry Carrier.

It was painted in the exact same desert camouflage pattern as ours. The serial numbers stenciled on the side armor were identical. But it looked like it had been through a meat grinder. The armor was scorched, peppered with heavy caliber bullet holes, and half the vehicle was phasing in and out of reality, glitching like a bad video file.

Thorne walked up to the crumpled hood of the opposing Stryker, her revolver drawn. The driver’s side hatch was blown open. Inside the cabin, illuminated by the flickering red emergency light, slumped a dead soldier in a tactical uniform.

Thorne reached in and grabbed the soldier by the tactical vest, pulling him out so his face caught the beam of our flashlights.

Captain Miller, standing right next to me, let out a choked gasp and took a step back, his rifle trembling in his hands.

The dead soldier in the wrecked Stryker was Captain Miller.

His face was covered in dried blood, his eyes wide in a frozen stare of terror. His uniform bore the exact same name tape.

"It's a mirror," Julian whispered, staring at the dead doppelgänger. "The other universe. They tried to run the cleavage furrow, too."

Thorne looked at the dead Miller, then at the living Miller standing beside her. Her expression didn't change, but her grip on the revolver tightened until her knuckles were stark white.

"The cytoplasm is mixing," Thorne said, her voice dropping to a deadly whisper. "The universes are bleeding into each other. If their convoy failed... we are walking into whatever killed them."
