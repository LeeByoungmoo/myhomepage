Episode 17: The Multiverse Gate
The sliver of reality we stood on had been lopped off by a cosmic guillotine.

There was no wind. There wasn't even sound. The ear-splitting spatial friction, the roar of the diesel engine, and the echoes of the firefight that had been ringing just moments ago had completely evaporated. Beyond the hundred-yard chunk of mud and broken rock we stood on, a sea of perfect, absolute white noise gaped open.

There was no up, no down. Where the sky should have been, where the ground should have continued, there was only a static abyss. We were marooned on a severed umbilical cord, stranded in the gap between two newborn universes.

"Is this it?" Captain Miller muttered, his voice hollow. As he lowered his rifle and stepped toward the edge, Dr. Thorne yanked his shoulder back hard.

"Don't go near the edge, Captain," Thorne warned coldly. "That isn't a vacuum out there. It's a null state. If you take one step, your mass will scatter, unrendered forever."

Silas was already kneeling in the dirt, holding Julian up. The hard old prepper's calloused hands were shaking.

"Julian. Can you hear me? Stay with me, kid!" Silas gently slapped his grandson's cheek.

Julian looked horrific. The pixelated glow of the ghost data had burst through the gauze and snaked past his elbow, crawling toward his shoulder. His flesh flickered between our dimension and the other like a radio drifting off its station.

"I can't... breathe..." Julian gasped in agony, clutching his chest. "Feels like... broken glass in my lungs."

"Of course it does," Thorne said callously, checking her magazine. "Half of your body currently belongs to the multiverse we just detached from. You are being physically torn apart, Dr. Mercer."

"Shut your mouth!" Silas growled, violently grabbing his shotgun. "My grandson is dying! Arthur, you said you knew the mechanism! You're the biologist. Tell me what the next step in this fucked-up cell division is!"

Ice water ran down my spine. My brain wanted to deny it, but everything I had spent my life learning about biology was forcing a brutal truth on me.

"Silas..." my voice trembled violently. "There is no... next step."

Silas's blue eyes narrowed fiercely. "What?"

I pointed to the wall of absolute nothingness surrounding us. "When a cell finishes dividing, the midbody—the last thread connecting the two daughter cells—gets cut loose. We're standing on the scrap."

"Then what happens to this chunk of land?" Chloe asked, her voice thick with tears.

"A cell doesn't leave debris lying around," I said, swallowing hard. "Phagocytosis. Autophagy. Enzymes move in and break down what's left of the midbody. They digest it completely and recycle the parts."

As soon as the words left my mouth, the edges of our island began to give way with a wet, grinding sound. Rock and mud boiled along the borders, then dissolved pixel by pixel into the white noise.

Our hundred-yard island shrank to ninety yards. The abyss was starting to 'digest' us.

"Dammit!" Miller backed away.

Thorne didn't panic. She holstered her revolver, drew a heavy titanium survival knife from her vest, and strode straight toward Julian.

"What the hell do you think you're doing?" Silas blocked her, raising his shotgun.

"Move, old man." Thorne's eyes were cold and calculating. "That arm is an anchor. The glitch is dragging the pressure of the parallel universe toward us and speeding up the collapse. The arm comes off. It's the only way he lives, and the only way we buy a few more minutes."

"You lay one finger on my grandson, and I'll blow your head off."

"Silas, she's right," Julian gasped, coughing up blood. He looked down at his glitching arm with disgust. "Cut it off. This thing is dragging me into the other dimension. Do it."

Silas's face twisted in agony. As he slowly lowered the shotgun, Thorne knelt beside Julian and used the tip of the blade to find the line where the glitch met healthy flesh.

The tourniquets had gone down with the Strykers. How would we stop the arterial bleeding? A dozen survival scenarios flashed through my mind. But then my eyes locked onto Julian's pixelated fingertips.

They weren't flickering at random. They had a direction. Even as the island crumbled and the noise closed in from every side, the particles streaming off Julian's fingertips were being drawn toward one specific point in the static wall.

Like iron filings drawn to a magnet.

"Wait!" I yelled, grabbing Thorne's wrist.

"Let go, Dr. Finch. We're out of time," Thorne frowned.

"You can't cut it! If you do, we all die here!" I pointed frantically at Julian's fingertips. "His arm isn't corrupted. It's *entangled*! Half his body belongs to the new universe, which means that arm is still bound to its physics!"

"How the hell does that help us?" Captain Miller screamed, watching the island shrink to eighty yards.

"It means that arm is a door," I said, looking straight into Julian's eyes. "Julian, your arm is the only thing out here still connected to the other world. It knows where it belongs. It's a compass."

"Arthur... what are you talking about..."

"When you reached into the tear, you pulled Chloe back to this side," I shouted, gripping his good shoulder. "Now do the opposite. Reach into that wall. Use your quantum signature to grab hold of the other universe and pry the gap open!"

Thorne's eyes widened as she understood my meaning. "That's insane. You want to use biological mass to physically tear open a dimensional wall?"

"It's the only way out of here before the universe digests us."

The island was now less than fifty yards across. A massive redwood tree dissolved into pixels with a crackle of static and vanished into the void. The abyss was right at our throats.

Julian looked at me, his face streaked with sweat and tears. Swallowing his pain, he slowly nodded. Silas supported his back, helping him stand.

We stumbled to the edge of the island. A blinding wall of white noise churned inches from our faces.

"Do it, Julian," I whispered.

Julian gritted his teeth and slowly reached his static-laced, translucent right arm out toward the wall of noise in the void.

The moment his fingertips touched the barrier, a massive spark erupted, and the same bone-deep, low-frequency groan we'd heard in the bunker rolled out of the void. Julian screamed. His entire arm blazed with light.

"Grab it!" I shouted. "Grab the mass of the other universe and pull!"

Julian plunged his hand through the invisible wall. It was like watching a man push his arm through setting glass. The muscles in his shoulder corded, veins standing out like cables. He gripped something immensely heavy, let out a primal roar, and threw his whole body backward.

There was a long, shrieking tear, like canvas ripping under impossible tension, and then a concussion that punched the air out of my lungs.

With the sound of physical laws tearing, a rift opened right in the middle of the perfect wall of white noise.

And through that rift, we saw it.

Beyond the screaming static was a deep blue sky. Unfamiliar trees swayed in the wind, and warm, peaceful sunlight spilled through the gap. I swear I could hear birds. It was the newborn universe on the far side of the storm. The B-side.

"It's closing!" Chloe screamed. Julian was pouring every ounce of strength he had into holding the rift open, but the universe was relentless. The torn edges began knitting themselves shut from both sides.

The island was now only twenty yards across. The mud beneath our feet was crumbling away.

"Jump!" Thorne commanded.

Silas and Miller threw themselves through first. Chloe followed. Thorne glanced back at me for half a second, then vanished into the blue sky without a word.

"Julian! Let's go!" I grabbed his left arm and pushed him toward the rift.

But Julian didn't move. He was staring at his right arm, the one holding the gap open. The glitch had crawled all the way up the back of his neck.

"Arthur," Julian said in a calm, subdued voice. "I can't go."

"What the hell are you talking about? Jump, now!" I yanked his arm frantically.

"This arm can only open the door because I'm a glitch." Julian's face was twisted in pain, but his blue eyes were as calm as deep space. "When the cut came, the rest of you got sorted—assigned to one side, clean. I didn't. I had my hand in the tear. Half of my data is already written into the Julian over there. The second I cross, Pauli exclusion kicks in. Two of me in the same state. Annihilation. If I go through... the blast takes all of you with me."

It hit me like a hammer. The math was perfect. He was a duplicate file. He couldn't cross.

The mud beneath our feet crumbled into the abyss. The island was down to a speck, barely five yards across.

"I'll hold the door until the second you're through." Julian grinned. It was the same grin he'd worn on that slate roof the night my father smashed his telescope. "So please, Artie. This time, run away from the microscope."

My vision blurred. Every rational instinct I had as a scientist was coming apart.

A heartbeat before the island vanished, I stepped back and threw myself into the rift.

In the last fraction of a second, as the storm of light swallowed me, I looked back through the closing door. Julian was smiling as he dissolved into the perfect white noise of nothing.

And the door closed. The division was complete.
