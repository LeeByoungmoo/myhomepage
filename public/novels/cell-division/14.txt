Episode 14: Doppelgänger
Captain Miller didn't scream. He didn't panic. He just stared down at his own dead, blood-streaked face inside the wrecked Stryker, his mind completely failing to process the ontological horror of the moment.

Dr. Evelyn Thorne, however, was already moving. She holstered her revolver and pulled a small tactical flashlight from her vest. She leaned into the crushed cabin, ignoring the flickering, glitching edges of the parallel vehicle, and focused her beam on the dead Miller’s chest.

"He didn't die from the impact," Thorne said, her voice cutting through the humid forest air with surgical precision. She pointed to two neat, dark holes in the center of the dead man’s Kevlar plate. "Double tap, center mass. 5.56 millimeter. Standard-issue NATO rounds."

She stepped back, her eyes scanning the dark, mist-filled redwood forest. "They weren't killed by the environment, Captain. They were ambushed. By our own weapons."

I felt a cold dread pooling in my stomach. The biological mechanism was aligning perfectly, and it was terrifying.

"Julian," I whispered, grabbing his arm. "The Pauli exclusion principle. What happens when two identical fermions are forced into the exact same quantum state?"

"Total quantum annihilation," Julian replied instantly, his eyes widening as he caught my train of thought.

"The cytoplasm is mixing," I said, gesturing to the impossible forest around us. "The pressure inside this spatial bottleneck is immense. The universe is trying to squeeze two complete timelines into a single physical corridor before the cell membrane seals. But the hard drive is full."

"So it has to delete the duplicate files," Julian breathed.

"Exactly. It's a biological pruning mechanism. If we occupy the same spatial coordinates as our duplicates when the division finalizes, the paradox will annihilate both of us."

Thorne yanked back the charging handle of her rifle. "So, their universe is collapsing, and they realized the only way to survive the quantum overlap is to make sure they are the only versions of us left."

CRACK!

The sharp, supersonic snap of a high-velocity rifle round echoed through the trees. Sparks showered from the hull of our Stryker as the bullet ricocheted inches from my head.

"Contact!" Miller roared, his paralysis breaking. He tackled Chloe to the muddy ground, raising his M4.

"Defensive perimeter!" Thorne barked, dropping behind the massive tires of Silas's Stryker. "Return fire!"

Silas didn't need orders. The old prepper was already moving, his Remington 870 roaring to life. The heavy buckshot shredded the giant ferns fifty yards into the tree line.

From the dense fog, muzzle flashes strobed like erratic lightning. The air was filled with the deafening chatter of assault rifles and the splintering of redwood bark. I was pinned in the mud beside Julian, my hands over my head, feeling the concussive shockwaves of the firefight vibrating through my ribs.

"Cease fire! Cease fire!" a voice echoed from the trees.

It was amplified by a tactical megaphone. But it wasn't a stranger's voice.

It was Dr. Evelyn Thorne’s voice.

The gunfire slowly ground to a halt, leaving only the ringing in our ears and the hiss of ruptured radiators. Out of the thick, emerald mist, a figure stepped forward.

She wore the same black tactical uniform. She held the same mechanical compass. But her uniform was torn, soaked in blood, and her left arm was hanging limply at her side. Her edges were flickering slightly, glitching with static as the universe struggled to render her existence in our timeline.

It was Thorne. The B-side Thorne.

Behind her, emerging from the shadows of the giant redwoods, were half a dozen surviving soldiers from the parallel convoy. They looked battered, desperate, and terrifyingly familiar.

"Evelyn," the parallel Thorne called out, her voice rasping with exhaustion. She kept her rifle lowered, but her good hand rested near the trigger. "You know the physics. You know the math. The dimensional tear is closing behind us, and the safe zone is too narrow for two sets of data."

Our Thorne stood up slowly from behind the Stryker, her rifle leveled squarely at her doppelgänger's chest. "You hesitated at the bridge, didn't you? Your driver hesitated. That’s why your convoy crashed. Your timeline is the corrupted one."

Parallel Thorne offered a grim, bloody smile. "We’re the anomaly. I know. Our spatial geometry is already fraying. We lost thirty men to the static in the last hour. But if we eliminate you... if we clear the quantum coordinates... the universe will have to accept us as the primary data."

"You're a glitch," our Thorne said, her voice devoid of any empathy. "You’re a biological redundancy trying to survive a system wipe."

"I am you, Evelyn," the parallel Thorne said, taking a slow step forward. "I have your memories. I have your pragmatism. I calculated the exact same survival odds you did. You know I won't hesitate to kill you to save my convoy."

Our Thorne didn't blink. She didn't lower her weapon.

"You're right," our Thorne said smoothly. "We run the exact same calculations."

BANG.

Thorne didn't use the rifle. With lightning speed, she had drawn the heavy revolver from her hip and fired a single, deafening shot.

The heavy .357 magnum round caught the parallel Thorne dead in the center of her chest.

The doppelgänger staggered backward, her eyes wide with shock. But she didn't just bleed. The physical trauma destabilized her quantum state. The bullet hole erupted in a shower of blinding white static, spreading across her body like burning paper. In less than three seconds, the parallel Thorne dissolved into a cloud of pixelated dust and vanished, completely deleted from reality.

"Open fire!" our Thorne screamed, diving back into the mud.

The forest erupted. Without their commander, the desperate parallel soldiers opened up with everything they had.

"Get in the trucks!" Silas roared, pumping his shotgun and blowing a parallel soldier back into the mist. "We can't hold them! There’s a whole goddamn company of them out there!"

I scrambled on my hands and knees through the mud, dragging Chloe toward the rear hatch of our Stryker. Julian was right behind me. Bullets were sparking off the armor, filling the air with the smell of vaporized lead.

We threw ourselves into the dark cabin. Miller bounded in a second later, grabbing the radio. "Driver, punch it! Run them over if you have to! Move!"

The diesel engine screamed. The heavy Stryker lurched forward, its massive tires tearing through the mud and crushing the giant ferns. I looked through the viewing slit as we accelerated.

The parallel soldiers were chasing us, their figures flickering and glitching in the red taillights. They weren't just fighting for their lives; they were fighting for their right to exist.

Thorne climbed into the cabin through the top hatch, slamming it shut and locking the heavy steel bolts. She leaned against the vibrating bulkhead, her face splattered with mud, her breathing heavy but controlled.

She looked down at the heavy revolver in her hand.

"Dr. Finch," Thorne said, her eyes meeting mine in the dim red light. "Tell me there aren't any more phases after this."

"There aren't," I whispered, the horror of what she had just done—what we were all a part of—sinking in. "This is the final bottleneck. From here on out, it’s survival of the fittest."
