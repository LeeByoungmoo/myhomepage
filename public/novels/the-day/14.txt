Worship of Pain
After Professor Harrison's mechanical room was destroyed, the air in the camp completely changed. The false hope that 'we can create life with our own power' was exposed to the entire camp within a few days. When the terminal sentence was handed down that humanity's expiration date, no, this hell's expiration date, was 100 years at most, something massive permanently snapped inside the men.

In a world where the future had vanished, there was no reason to endure today for 'tomorrow'. But what drove humans the craziest was not hunger or violence, but a terrible **'absence of sensation'**.

Instead of going out to find food, Jason headed to the third basement floor of the apartment. It was a large open space once used as an indoor golf driving range for residents. As he approached, he heard dull striking sounds and beast-like cries. Left uncontrolled by Carter's red armbands, the place had now become a secret and bizarre 'arena' for the men in the camp.

But what Jason witnessed was not a fight to steal resources. Two men were standing face to face, swinging weapons. One man used a rusty box cutter to make a long slash across the other's forearm. With a *sliiice-* sound, the skin gaped open deeply.

If it were a normal living creature, he should have screamed and clutched the wound. However, the man whose arm was cut widened his eyes and peered into the wound as if digging into it.

"Why... why doesn't it hurt? Why isn't there any blood!" The man screamed, frantically digging into his own wound with his fingers. Inside the deep wound, instead of bright red blood, only dry, ash-grey muscle fibers and dark oozing liquid fell like dust.

"Stab it harder! I'm alive! If I'm alive, it should hurt!" The man grabbed the other by the collar and screamed. The man holding the box cutter also whimpered and stabbed his own thigh deeply. The terrifying sound of the blade hitting bone echoed, but instead of a look of pain, only a thick emptiness spread across his face.

It was **'Worship of Pain'**. Men, who used to have their existence validated in front of the gaze of the 'other' (women), were experiencing a horrific existential crisis of becoming transparent once the mirror disappeared.

Descartes said, 'I think, therefore I am,' but for these beasts who had fallen to the absolute bottom, the final method of proof left was **'I feel pain, therefore I am alive.'** To confirm they were alive, to prove that their bodies still belonged to the living world, they tore each other's flesh and burned it with fire, begging for sensation.

In the corner of the golf range, there were those who lit candles and burned their fingers in the flame, and those who rammed their heads into concrete walls until they cracked. But their eyes were all equally empty. The nerve cells had stopped, and the body, while being destroyed, sent no warning signals (pain) to the brain.

A bizarre self-harm party of corpses unable to suffer. Watching the miserable scene, Jason reached a chilling realization.

'Carter's rule won't end because of an external rebellion.'

The only means by which Carter could rule this camp with an iron fist was 'fear and pain through violence'. But what if those being ruled felt no pain, and instead started begging for greater pain to feel alive?

The moment punishment degenerated into a reward, and violence failed to inflict any biological damage, the chair Carter sat on would become nothing more than a ridiculous block of wood. The rules of this camp were already collapsing from the roots.

"Hey, Jason." Someone called Jason from the darkness. Turning around, he saw a neighbor man whose ear was half cut off and covered in a blood scab. In his hand, he held a dirty, bloodless kitchen knife.

"Do you want to try too? If you stab here... you feel a very faint, tingling sensation. You can feel that you're not dead yet, that your heart is beating. Should I stab you?"

The man approached with unfocused eyes and held out the kitchen knife. With an expressionless face, Jason lightly batted the man's wrist away. The kitchen knife fell limply to the floor.

"Stop it." Jason's voice was colder and drier than the chilly air of the basement. "No matter how much you tear flesh and break bones, the sensation you want will never return."

"What? How do you know that!"

"Because dead meat has no pain receptors."

Jason turned his back on the man and walked away. There was no need to waste any more time in this arena of crazy phantoms. Without realizing it, Jason's hand was stroking the cold handle of the hunting knife in his pants pocket.

There was no need for the stupid act of begging others for pain. The moment of the 'first awakening'—to perfectly prove the physical truth that he was already dead and this body was nothing but an illusion—was quietly boiling at the bottom of Jason's frozen heart.
