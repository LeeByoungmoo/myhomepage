The Shadowless
Jason left the 3rd basement floor that had turned into an arena and returned to his apartment, unit 802. In the living room with the electricity cut off, only the cold moonlight came through the window, drawing a rectangular blue chill on the floor.

Jason stood blankly in the middle of the living room where the moonlight fell brightest. The crazy self-harm show of the men he had seen in the basement clung to his retinas and wouldn't peel off. Bodies that didn't bleed even when flesh was torn, and didn't feel pain even when bones were scraped. They dismissed the bizarre phenomenon merely as 'paralysis of sensation' and struggled to somehow deceive their brains.

But Jason was different. His reason, which handled data and variables, was deriving the most horrific result value.

He pulled the heavy hunting knife from his pants pocket. The cool blade drawn from its sheath gleamed terrifyingly in the moonlight. Jason opened his left hand. It was the familiar hand that had once held Sarah's warm hand, tied the laces of Chloe's pale pink sneakers, and typed out tens of thousands of lines of Python code.

Jason neither took a deep breath nor squeezed his eyes shut. With an expressionless face, he brought the sharp tip of the hunting knife to the center of his left palm. And without hesitation, very slowly and deeply, he drew it across his palm.

*Sliiiice-*

An unpleasant tearing sound, like tough fabric ripping, rang through the quiet living room. The skin split open to both sides. It was a wound deep enough to expose bone. It was a fatal injury that would have a normal human rolling on the floor screaming.

But Jason stood motionless in place and looked down at his palm. The red, hot blood that should have gushed from between the parted flesh was nowhere to be found. Instead, like a cracked mud floor in a drought, only bloodless, ash-grey muscle fibers were dryly exposed. Because the heart had stopped pumping, not a single drop of blood was circulating in the blood vessels.

There was not even pain. The nerve cells did not send electrical signals to the brain announcing an emergency. There was only the 'visual information' that he had been cut by a knife; Jason's body produced no biological reaction whatsoever.

"I knew it."

A low, dry sigh escaped Jason's parched lips. He absentmindedly traced the gap in his palm with a finger. Dead skin cells crumbling like dust fell to the floor. This perfect lack of pain (無痛) and lack of blood (無血) was irrefutable evidence proving that this body was a 'corpse' whose life activities had completely stopped a long time ago.

Jason slowly bowed his head and looked at his feet. The moonlight pouring through the window cast the shadows of the living room sofa and table thick and black on the floor. The objects were clearly blocking the light and proving their existence with shadows.

But Jason's own shadow, standing directly in the moonlight, was different. The shadow that should have started from his toes was so bizarrely faint that its shape was unrecognizable. Like light passing through translucent cellophane, Jason's shadow barely left a trace on the floor before crumbling. Even the physical laws of the world were rejecting his already dead existence and letting the light pass right through him.

The shadowless. The body that cannot bleed.

Jason let out a small, chilling laugh. The laugh gradually grew into a bizarre guffaw that echoed through the entire living room. It wasn't despair. It was a terrifying **'sense of liberation'** that only someone who perfectly understood the true stage props of this horrific play could feel.

The world didn't end because the women disappeared. On the morning their wives stopped breathing, the husbands, the sons, and all the men in this city also had their hearts stop simultaneously. It was just that a terrible 'inertia' remained in the aimless bodies, and they were wandering the living world, dragging rotting bodies around while the brain failed to recognize death.

We are all dead. Carter, the red armbands, and the ones tearing flesh in the arena, all of them.

"A camp of the dead."

Jason grabbed the empty air with his wounded hand that did not bleed. How shallow and ridiculous was the violence and fear that Carter wielded while ruling from his chair. How on earth do you control someone who is already dead by killing them again? The times they put their lives on the line for a few cans of food and willingly acted as slaves, trembling in fear of being beaten to death by a steel pipe, felt like a massive comedy.

In Jason's empty eyes, a sharp gleam—different from vitality—flashed for the first time. Now, he had become the only **'awakened one in a lucid dream'** in this camp who realized he was a ghost.

He picked up the hunting knife dropped on the floor again. Now it was time to close the door to this nauseating purgatory. It was time to drag down that arrogant king, Carter, who believed the power he held ruled over life, and carve into his eyes the truth that he, too, was nothing but a miserable ghost.

The moment Jason opened the door and stepped out into the hallway, a roar erupted with a massive tearing sound from outside the quiet apartment complex. To smash the shell of the camp trapped in an illusion, the real hell from outside was breaking down the doors and surging in. The curtain of a massive catastrophe, Act 4, was rising.
