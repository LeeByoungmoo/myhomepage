The Coldest Morning
The familiar, cheerful electronic chirp of the alarm clock shattered the silence. The smartphone screen glowing in the dark read 6:30 AM. On any normal day, Jason would have dragged his heavy eyelids open, headed to the bathroom for a quick ten-minute shower, and started his routine. 

Jason reached out out of habit, silenced the alarm, and rolled over. Beneath the thin blanket, he saw the curve of his wife Sarah’s back. She was always one to beg for five more minutes, pulling the covers over her head to hide from the morning light.

“Sarah, it’s morning. Time to get up,” Jason murmured, his voice thick with sleep, gently shaking her shoulder.

There was no answer. Not even a slight stir.

“Are you that tired from working late yesterday?”

He reached out to shake her again, but his fingertips flinched and froze. The sensation through her thin pajamas was grotesque. Cold. No, it wasn’t just a simple chill—it was the temperature of an inanimate object, completely drained of life.

“Sarah?”

Anxiety crawled up his spine like a snake. Jason bolted upright and turned her body over. Her face was ashen. No breath escaped her half-parted lips. With trembling hands, Jason placed a finger under her nose, then unbuttoned her shirt and pressed his ear to her chest.

Silence. A perfect, absolute void of sound.

The flesh, which just hours ago had blood coursing through its veins, had stiffened to a terrifying degree. Frozen in terror, Jason let out a guttural sound and scrambled backward.

“S-Sarah! Honey! Open your eyes!”

He frantically grabbed his phone and dialed 911. It didn’t even ring. Only an automated voice coldly hammered against his ear: *“All circuits are busy. Please try your call again later.”*

That was when he heard it. A piercing scream outside the window. And it wasn’t just one. From the apartment below, from the building across the street, from the roads outside the complex—dozens, hundreds of screams and wails erupted simultaneously. 

Jason practically flew out to the living room. Everything was exactly as it had been last night: his jacket tossed on the sofa, the empty beer cans on the table.

*Bang! Bang! Bang!* The front door shuddered violently. “Jason! Are you in there?! Open the door! Please!”

It was Mike, the college student who lived next door. When Jason opened the door with trembling hands, a deathly pale Mike collapsed onto the floor, weeping. “Man… my mom… my mom back in my hometown isn’t answering… my sister isn't answering either… They say everyone is dead! Everyone is dead!”

Before Jason could ask what he meant, the TV in the corner—which Sarah had set to a sleep timer the night before—flickered on, spewing out breaking news.

The anchor on screen was a man. His usually slicked-back hair was a mess, his tie half-undone. The seat next to him, where his female co-anchor should have been, was glaringly empty.

“V-Viewers… Currently, an inexplicable… an unbelievable event is unfolding worldwide.” The anchor’s voice trembled. Tears fell openly onto the cue sheet in his hands. “According to foreign reports and local police… as of early this morning, all women on Earth… have simultaneously passed away. The cause is unknown, and… there are no reported survivors.”

Jason dropped his phone. It hit the floor with a dull thud. On the TV, footage showed streets gridlocked with crashed cars and men sobbing uncontrollably while clinging to ambulances. The deafening noise of a collapsing world poured through the living room windows. 

But in the midst of that extreme chaos, Jason felt a bizarre cognitive dissonance. The world had just ended, and his beloved wife was dead, yet a heavy, suffocating silence seemed to flow through his own ears. He couldn't hear his own heartbeat, which should have been pounding out of his chest. He wasn't even sweating.

He slumped to the floor, staring blankly at his two hands—the very hands that had just touched Sarah’s icy skin. It felt like he was a bystander watching a horrific disaster movie from outside the screen. 

It was the coldest morning, the day half the world evaporated. Without warning, the doomsday clock had stopped forever, trapping the dead and the leftover ghosts in the same frozen moment.
