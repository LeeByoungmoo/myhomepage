The Mary in the Moldy Dressing Room
Jason escaped unit 802, where he felt he was suffocating, and walked down the stairs aimlessly. To cool his mind, which was crushed by the digital illusion in the laptop, he needed a cool place where the light couldn't reach. His steps naturally headed toward the deepest basement storage room of building 104.

At the end of the hallway lined with storage rooms, an iron door that should have been firmly shut was open just a tiny crack. An alien smell leaked through that gap. It wasn't the smell of blood, nor the rotting sweat of widowers. It was a thick, artificial fragrance, a bizarre mixture of the moldy basement smell, cheap powder, and high-end perfume whose alcohol had half-evaporated.

Jason approached silently and peeked inside through the crack in the door. The room was a completely different world from the savage camp outside. Red velvet curtains, scavenged from somewhere, covered the walls, and battery-operated yellow fairy lights created a dreamy, warm-colored lighting.

And on an old sofa in the middle of the room, a 'person' wearing a red silk dress sat demurely.

It was Dylan, a young man in his twenties who used to work as a barista at the cafe in front of the complex before the event. He wore a loose cardigan to hide his thick frame, and he had slathered thick foundation as if plastering it on to hide his bluish beard shadow. The crookedly applied red lipstick and the sloppy bob wig were crude beyond measure, but the fragrance hovering in the room and the dim lighting bizarrely neutralized the grotesqueness.

However, what shocked Jason even more was the identity of the man lying with his head on Dylan's lap. It was Mike of the red armbands, Carter's right-hand man who swung a steel pipe, stole rations from the elderly, and committed violence during the day. Mike, who always lived with vulgar curses on his lips and bloodshot eyes, exhibiting nihilism and destructive instincts, was surprisingly now curled up like a child, burying his face in the hem of Dylan's dress and sobbing.

"You had a really hard time out there again today, didn't you... It's okay. Everything is going to be okay."

Dylan whispered, slowly stroking Mike's rough hair. It wasn't his usual masculine voice, but a 'female intonation', consciously raised in pitch and endlessly softened.

"I... I beat up old Mr. Henderson from the other building today. If I didn't, Boss Carter would have beaten me to death... I wanted to live... I was scared..." Mike made a sound like a sick beast and hugged Dylan's waist tightly.

"I know. It's not your fault. You just tried your best to survive. You're not a bad person. You can be forgiven for everything. After you get a good sleep here, tomorrow will be a little better."

Dylan endlessly agreed, empathized, and comforted. It was precisely that **'emotional labor'** and **'invisible care'** that women used to provide to neutralize the rough world of men in the past.

Outwardly, the men in the camp fought for hierarchy with violence and strength, pretending to be strong predators, but inside, they were rotting with terrible guilt and fear. In a hell where you get trampled if you don't trample others, they desperately needed the embrace of a 'mother' or 'wife' where they could show their vulnerability and be comforted without being questioned.

In exchange for a few cans of food, Dylan willingly volunteered to play the role of the 'Fake Mary', serving as an emotional trash can for those beasts and listening to their confessions.

Jason stood at the crack of the door and watched that sad play. Mike wasn't hugging Dylan, a cross-dresser, because he mistook him for a real woman. He knew about Dylan's thick Adam's apple and the chunky ankles revealed under the dress. Just as New Mexico whiptail lizards, bound by the inertia of the past without males, simulate fake mating between females, men, including Mike, thoroughly deceived their own brains and closed their eyes. They merely needed the 'illusion of women' itself to stop their collapse; the authenticity of the shell was not important.

When Mike fell asleep exhausted after crying for a long time, Dylan stared into thin air and let out a deep sigh. And he met Jason's eyes through the crack in the door. Dylan didn't scream, nor was he ashamed. He merely offered an exhausted smile and moved his lips, the lipstick smudged.

Seen up close, Dylan's face was disastrous. His skin, cracking through the thickly applied foundation, was an ashen color without a single drop of blood circulating. The flesh was dryly crumbling, and behind the smell of cosmetics, the unmistakable stench of death seeped out. Dylan, too, was nothing but a shell losing its body heat.

A world where a dying corpse, thickly plastered with makeup, mimics warmth and comforts another dying corpse, saying 'it's okay'.

Jason silently closed the door. What was scarier than physical hunger was spiritual starvation. The young man who turned himself into a clown to fill the massive emotional void left by women, and the weak, cruel males who had to seek comfort even if it meant being held in those fake arms just to keep from going insane.

Walking out into the dark hallway, Jason leaned his forehead against the wall. The chatbot on the laptop screen, and the man offering fake comfort with makeup on, were no different. Everyone was desperately mimicking life to deny the fact that they were already dead. This terrible play in the moldy dressing room was much more cruel and bitterly sad than the camp outside where steel pipes were swung.
