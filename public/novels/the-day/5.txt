Red Armbands and the Ruler on the Chair
The era of chaotic violence did not last long. Barbarism always evolves the most efficient and cruel control system under the guise of survival.

Less than a month after the women disappeared, a massive iron fence and barricade were erected in the first-floor lobby of building 104 where Jason lived. The doors of abandoned cars, broken furniture, and wire mesh ripped from the mart were tangled together, blocking the entrance to the fortress.

And right in the center of that barricade, an abandoned vintage armchair sat alone like a throne.

"Line up! Anyone who cuts in line is getting their skull bashed in!"

With a sharp scream, the metallic screech of a steel pipe dragging across the floor echoed through the lobby. The owner of the voice was Mike, the college student who lived next door to Jason. Just a few days ago, he was wailing in nihilism while burning his major textbooks; now, wearing a **'red armband'** on his forearm, he was brandishing a weapon.

A dozen or so young men wearing red armbands like Mike had taken over the apartment lobby. They herded the elderly and weak men like animals and searched their belongings. If anyone showed even a hint of resistance, a wooden club flew at them without mercy. When the social buffer of empathy and mediation (women) evaporated, the collective of males instantly plummeted past the most primal logic of power, 'the war of all against all', straight into a **'fascist hierarchy'**.


Jason stood in the ration line, clutching the three remaining cans of Spam in his bag. His eyes were drawn to a man sitting cross-legged in the armchair in front of the iron fence.

It was Carter, the creator of this new order and the leader of the red armbands.

Before the event, Carter had run a small gym in the local commercial district. He was just one of the loud, muscular 'machos' commonly found in any neighborhood. In a capitalist society, his physical strength had been thoroughly controlled by money and power. But in a world where laws and currency had disappeared, and only 'physical force' became the sole currency, Carter was a fish perfectly in water.

"Listen up, you rats." Carter leaned back in his chair and growled low. Even without a speaker, his voice was heavy and imposing enough to press down on the entire lobby.

"The outside world is already hell. If you want to get stabbed while looking for food, you can crawl out of this iron fence right now. But if you want to live, follow my rules."

When Carter snapped his fingers, Mike dragged an old man and threw him at Carter's feet. It was an old man who had been caught trying to secretly hide a can of tuna. Without even standing up, Carter stomped on the old man's head with his combat boot.

"Any food or medicine you have is confiscated. I will distribute it as rations. Those who can work will repair the barricades, and the old and sick will go down to the underground parking lot and stay out of sight. Those who follow my control will be spared, and those who rebel..."

As Carter applied pressure to his combat boot, the old man screamed. "...I will throw you outside this barricade. You can starve to death or get eaten alive, I don't care."

It was the perfect logic of a gangster, and simultaneously the moment the most primitive form of a 'state' was born. A kingdom of beasts that monopolized violence and robbed individuals of all property and freedom under the pretext of protection.

Men, having lost the opportunity to prove themselves in the gaze of the 'other' (women), were now desperately inflating their shrunken egos by reigning over others and exercising violence. Carter's rule was beyond simply securing food; it was a horrifying struggle to confirm, 'I am still a living predator with teeth.'

Jason's turn arrived. He obediently opened his bag and handed over three cans of Spam to a red armband.

"Name," Mike asked gruffly, holding a ledger. 

"Jason. Building 104, Unit 802."

Mike glanced at Jason, smirked, and took the canned goods. Yesterday's neighbor had become today's warden, perfectly intoxicated by the power trip of his rank. After receiving the ration confirmation stamp on the back of his hand, Jason crossed the iron fence and headed toward the camp stairs leading to the underground parking lot.

Before descending the stairs, Jason slightly turned his head and stared once more at Carter sitting in the armchair. Carter was now holding the collar of the old man who had hidden food, roaring like a beast. His bulging neck veins and explosive muscles looked like living power itself.

But the reality of Carter reflected in Jason's eyes was different. The light from the emergency exit sign on the lobby ceiling was shining down behind Carter, but the shadow stretching beneath the armchair was as chillingly faint as Jason's own.

Jason let out a hollow chuckle. Whether you were the one wielding violence in power or the one cowering under it, it was all the same in the end.

'Play king all you want. It's just a camp of rotting ghosts anyway.'

Leaving behind the shouting of the red armband calling him, Jason slowly walked down into the deep darkness of the underground camp reeking of rot. In the place where the world's clock had stopped, the curtain was rising on a terrifyingly twisted, deformed ecosystem.
