Becoming Dust
Carter's hand, which had been holding onto Jason's ankle, slipped away and scattered like a parched sandcastle crumbling. The king, who once commanded the camp and believed himself to be a predator, became a handful of ashes first and disappeared into thin air, leaving behind only bitter repentance for the family he killed in his final moments.

Carter's extinction meant the loss of gravity that supported this massive purgatory. The hounds of the Matrix who had been screaming and firing guns, and the red armbands who had raised steel pipes, no longer attacked each other. The terrible survival instinct and violent nature were nothing more than a neurosis stemming from the illusion that 'they were alive'. As the curtain of that blind illusion lifted, a deep, distant fatigue settled in the empty eyes of the ghosts instead of anger.

"Is it... over." Mike, sitting on the floor, put down the bloodstained wooden club he was holding. His body, too, was turning into translucent ash-grey powder and crumbling away, starting from his fingertips. But Mike's face looked more peaceful than ever. The eternal relief that he no longer had to trample anyone, nor force himself to act strong, spread through his scattering smile.

The moldy dressing room in the basement storage. Dylan looked in the mirror, took off his crude wig, and vigorously wiped off the thickly applied foundation on his face with his sleeve. The fake warmth he had forced out to become someone's fake comfort was no longer needed. He slowly closed his eyes and seeped into the crumbling darkness of the basement.

The same went for Professor Harrison in the broken mechanical room. He no longer looked at the rotting somatic cells rolling on the floor. Instead, he watched with wondrous eyes as his two hands turned into dazzling dust and scattered into the air, finally accepting the most perfect and natural providence of the universe (death).

While all the phantoms of the camp shed their heavy shells of the living world, Jason stepped on the crumbling stairs and slowly walked upwards.

Building 104, Unit 802. The place where the times he loved were taxidermied. The front door had already lost its shape and become transparent.

Entering the living room, Jason saw the screen of the stopped laptop crumbling on its own and scattering into particles of light. Not the fake Sarah forcibly pieced together with data of 0s and 1s, but the door to the place he truly belonged was opening.

Jason stood in front of Chloe's pale pink sneakers placed in the corner of the entryway. He slowly knelt and carefully wrapped both hands around those small shoes. Surprisingly, from the sneakers that were always coldly chilled, a faint yet affectionate warmth was transmitted. It wasn't the fake body heat that the corpses imitated. It was the warmth of the soul that finally blooms when one lays down the heaviest burden of lingering attachment left in the living world.

"Sarah. Chloe."

Jason's parched lips moved. From his empty eyes that did not bleed blood, a miraculously clear, hot tear flowed down.

"Your words, 'Honey, you had a hard day today, didn't you...' were right. I, we... were lost and wandering for too long."

Jason slowly closed his eyes. Starting from the terrible gunshot wound pierced in his chest, his body began to turn into a powder of soft light and crumble. There was no pain. Rather, only a terribly refreshing sense of liberation enveloped his soul, as if he had dived deep underwater and finally surfaced to take his first breath.

"I'm clocking out now. I'm sorry I'm late."

With Jason's final whisper, even the last form containing his existence became a wisp of wind and scattered beyond the open balcony window. His daughter's small sneakers, the laptop containing tens of thousands of conversations, and the living room floor he was standing on all became dazzling particles and soared into the sky.

The concrete purgatory created by Carter's terrible guilt, the entire building 104 apartment complex, turned into a massive pillar of dust and collapsed. It was an extremely quiet and beautiful collapse, without even the sound of an explosion.

The clouds finally cleared over the city that had lost its colors, revealing a wide-open blue sky. Weeds grew through the asphalt on the empty roads, and from the stump of the broken cherry tree, blue sprouts of new life quietly poked their heads out.

The era of foolish ghosts who lost the mirror called the 'other' (他者), and tore each other's flesh and lusted after futile power to prove they were alive, thus came to an end. The final ending credits of the species called humanity did not conclude with a destructive war or pandemic, but with the terrible inertia holding onto the stopped clock finally exhausting its strength and fading into eternal rest.

The wind blew. The world was so quiet it couldn't be more perfect, and only the warm spring sunlight poured down brilliantly over the empty Earth where no one remained.
