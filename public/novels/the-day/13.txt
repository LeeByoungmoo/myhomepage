The Final Century
In front of the mechanical room in the apartment basement, the sound of someone pounding on the heavy iron door tore through the damp air of the basement.

"Professor Harrison! Open the door! Open it right now?!"

Carter screamed, kicking the iron door with his combat boot. Behind him stood Jason and the red armbands with anxious eyes.

For the past few days, Professor Harrison had locked the mechanical room door from the inside, even refusing food rations. The situation in the camp outside was reaching its limit. Food was running out, and as even the places to squeeze for loot disappeared, the men's eyes slowly began to turn toward Carter's neck. The only justification Carter had to suppress the energy of this riot was the 'miracle of life creation' that Professor Harrison held.

"Fuck, break it down."

At Carter's command, Mike and the burly men grabbed sledgehammers and smashed the iron door handle. *Bang, bang, ba-bang-!* Finally, the chunk of metal was torn off, and the door opened.

At that moment, the terrible gas that had pooled inside the mechanical room poured out. A perfect stench of death (사취, 屍臭) and formalin that made it impossible to breathe, the smell of rotting meat. The men gagged and covered their noses.

The mechanical room was a scene of chaos. The microscope he cherished so much was rolling on the floor, and all the glass tubes containing the culture fluid were shattered into pieces, soaking the floor stickily.

And right in the middle, Professor Harrison was crouching. He was holding a piece of broken glass tightly in his bare hands, giggling grotesquely in the darkness.

"Professor... What is all this?" Jason asked, approaching cautiously.


Professor Harrison slowly raised his head at Jason's voice. His face was half its size, and his pupils had sunk into a deep abyss of despair. When Professor Harrison opened his clenched hand, instead of blood, broken glass fragments mixed with black, dried-up pieces of flesh fell to the floor with a patter.

"It's over." Professor Harrison's voice sounded like dry autumn leaves crumbling.

"What do you mean it's over! You said the cells were dividing! You said we could make a new humanity!" Carter grabbed Professor Harrison by the collar and shook him violently.

"It was a lie... No, I went crazy and saw a hallucination." Hanging from Carter's strong grip, Professor Harrison laughed weakly. "Stem cells? Dedifferentiation? It's all bullshit. Even if we apply electricity and pour chemicals on them, these damn cells don't divide a single millimeter. They're just... just rotting. From the start, they were perfectly dead garbage meat with not even one percent of vitality left!"

"Shut your mouth! You failed! Use different chemicals and make them again!" For the first time, fear appeared in Carter's eyes. Professor Harrison's failure meant the extinction of the 'hope' that served as the foundation of Carter's power, which ruled the camp.

"You stupid bastard, do you still not get it?" Professor Harrison suddenly thrust his face into Carter's and shouted. "Do you know what happens to a species in the ecosystem that completely loses its ability to reproduce? It doesn't mean evolution has stopped. It just means we've become canned goods with an 'expiration date'!"

Professor Harrison kicked a broken petri dish away and screamed. "Let's say the youngest surviving infant right now lives the longest. 100 years at most. 100 years! We've received the most miserable terminal sentence in the universe. In 100 years, the human species on this Earth will go perfectly extinct. There is no next generation, so what the hell is the point of us sharing rations here, creating a hierarchy, and swinging steel pipes?!"

The final century. It was a definitive sentence crueler than death. A meaningless 100-year punishment where they had to wake up every morning and swallow rotting meat, knowing that it would end someday.

"This son of a bitch really...!" Losing his reason, Carter pulled the pistol from his waistband and struck Professor Harrison's head with it. *Smack!* With a dull thud, Professor Harrison rolled on the floor.

"Set it up again. Even if it's rotting water, put it back under the microscope! The bastards outside need hope. Even if it's fake, tell them life is growing!" Carter wailed like a beast and began to stomp on the fallen Professor Harrison with his combat boots.

A dictator forcing fake salvation to maintain his power. And a scientist who had gone completely mad in the face of the massive truth of biological extinction.

Jason took a step back among the red armbands and watched the gruesome scene. Under Carter's kicks, Professor Harrison's ribs were caving in and his jawbone was breaking. But there was not the slightest agitation in Jason's eyes.

No matter how brutally Carter stomped on him, not a single drop of blood splattered from Professor Harrison's mouth as he lay face down on the floor. Teeth that had fallen out through his broken jaw merely rattled and rolled on the dry cement floor; the mechanical room floor was chillingly dry.

*Professor.* Jason muttered inwardly, looking down at the dying Professor Harrison with a completely bloodless face.

*You gave us too generous an expiration date.*

100 years until human extinction? Even that was a luxurious number given only to living creatures. It wasn't because of a lack of technology that cell division stopped. From the start, applying electricity to pieces of flesh torn from a corpse could never birth life.

Jason stared at his own empty pupils reflected in a piece of broken glass. We are not going extinct in 100 years. We are phantoms who should have stopped breathing on that first morning when our wives and daughters disappeared. This underground bunker, where ghosts were looking into rotting meat claiming to create life, and breaking each other's bones claiming to seize power, was the perfect purgatory (煉獄).

The era of thin illusions that filled the mechanical room was shattered. All that remained was a meaningless hellscape of bloodless men, the end of which was unknown.
