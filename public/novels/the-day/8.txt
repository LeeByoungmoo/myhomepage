The Lost Daughter's Sneakers
Dragging his heavy feet, Jason opened the front door of unit 802. A cool air, welcoming no one, circled the house. Out of habit, Jason reached to take off his shoes, but his gaze stopped at a small pair of shoes placed neatly in a corner of the entryway.

Size 3. They were pale pink velcro sneakers, now covered in a fine layer of sand dust. They were the shoes of his daughter, Chloe, who had run the relay race at her elementary school sports day the day before the world stopped.

Jason slowly knelt and picked up those small shoes. Chloe, who wasn't very good at running, would always be out of breath and panting with her mouth wide open by the time she rounded the turning point. Beads of sweat would form on her forehead, and the sunlight pouring over her flushed red cheeks was dazzling.

Sweating, panting, that dynamic vitality where the heart felt like it would burst. That brilliant pulsation that only a living, breathing life could emit was packed inside those small sneakers. But now, that hot breath existed nowhere in this world.

The disappearance of adult women—wives and mothers—meant the evaporation of the foundation for maintaining daily life and emotional comfort. But the fact that **'young daughters'** were erased from the world brought a completely different dimension of ruin to the men.

To fathers, a daughter was the most harmless and pure being in the world, the 'absolute object of protection' they had to defend even if it meant sacrificing their own lives. In the minds of the males who had lost that object, the moral brake that they must not harm others, and even the emotion of compassion, had been perfectly castrated.

Jason set the sneakers down and approached the balcony window. Below the window, he saw the apartment complex playground, once filled with the laughter of children. A group of about a dozen people was gathered over the rusty slide and the weed-infested sandbox.

They were boys in their mid-to-late teens. In the early days of the event, adults like Carter had a minimum of sympathy for these young males. They gave them a little more rations or exempted them from hard labor. But in just a few months, having lost their mothers' unconditional care and the clumsy emotional exchange with girls their age, the boys who grew up in Carter's camp—where only steel pipes and fists were the law—had mutated into terrifying monsters.

A dull striking sound came from the middle of the playground. The boys stood in a circle, collectively beating something. As Jason narrowed his eyes to look, the thing crouching on the ground was not a beast, but an old man from another building who had come out to get rations.

"Aaargh! Please, spare me... I was wrong!"

Despite the old man's screams, the boys didn't stop. It was on a different level from the deviance or expressions of anger committed by delinquent youth in the past. There was no anger, sadistic pleasure, or guilt on the boys' faces. Like kicking an empty can on the street, they were just swinging their wooden clubs expressionlessly and mechanically.

Perfect psychopaths whose mirror neurons, which recognize the 'pain of the other', were completely destroyed. If adults were forcing themselves to become monsters struggling to survive, these boys were a 'new humanity' born naturally in a vacuum where the emotional buffer was zero. To them, violence wasn't even play; it was a meaningless daily act like breathing.

*Whack! Smack!* One of the boys struck the old man's head hard with a wooden club. The old man's body convulsed and went limp.

But what gave Jason the chills wasn't just the boys' cruelty. Even though the old man's head was cracked and his skin torn, it wasn't red blood that sprayed over the sandbox. Cloudy, dry dust like crushed cement powder rose from the old man's body. The boys also scratched at the wounds on each other's faces, but instead of the smell of blood, only moldy, ash-grey dead skin cells rubbed off on their cheeks.

A playground of phantoms, crashing and breaking their empty bodies against each other without even knowing they were dying, no, without even knowing they were already dead.

The boys tapped the unmoving old man a few times with their feet, then slung their steel pipes over their shoulders with no emotion and sauntered off in search of other prey.

Jason quietly closed the balcony window and drew the curtains. In a world where there was nothing left to protect, there weren't even growing children. In this sealed concrete purgatory, only shells castrated of emotion were evolving. Crouching on the living room floor as darkness fell, Jason fixed his gaze on Chloe's pale pink sneakers placed in the entryway.

In this grey hell that had lost its colors, those small sneakers were the only things shining sadly like a tombstone holding onto the phantom of a past that once lived and breathed.
