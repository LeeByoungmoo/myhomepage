Collapsed Credit and the Economics of Beasts
Half a month had passed since Sarah died. The streets were now filled with a horrendous stench rather than sorrow. The recycling bins had already exploded, and food waste bags, torn apart by stray cats, were rotting on the asphalt. In normal times, someone would have filed a complaint, the management office would have called a cleaning crew, and everything would have been neatly tidied up. But all those functions of 'maintenance' and 'care' had magically evaporated along with the absence of women.

Driven by hunger, Jason left his apartment. His refined hands, which until a few days ago had been typing Python code and analyzing data, were now tightly gripping a crude wooden club. His destination was the large supermarket in the nearby commercial district.

The entrance to the mart was already a war zone. The automatic doors were shattered, and unidentifiable bloodstains were baked into the floor. As he stepped inside, a bizarre landscape unfolded.

The fresh produce section was quite literally a graveyard of objects. Fruits that once boasted vibrant colors were bruised and covered in mold, and the meat at the butcher counter had turned black, giving off a horrific stench of rot. Men were skilled at 'production' and 'conquest', but they were fiercely ignorant when it came to how to preserve ingredients, process them, and 'take care' of daily life. Even looking at the rotting raw meat, they had no idea what to do with it, merely holding their noses as they walked past.

"Look here... this is real gold. It's a Rolex. Shouldn't this be worth at least ten cans of tuna?"

In a corner, a white-haired, elderly gentleman was clinging to a younger man. It was Mr. Henderson, a retired bank branch manager known for his dignity at the local senior center. He had taken off his heavy watch and was offering it, begging.

The young man scoffed and shoved Mr. Henderson away. "Old man, have you lost your mind? Are you gonna start a fire with that chunk of metal? Are you gonna boil it for soup? Do you even know what the most expensive thing in this mart is right now?"

The place the man pointed to was unexpected. The feminine hygiene aisle. The shelves where sanitary pads should have been stacked were completely empty. In a world where women had disappeared, sanitary pads had been reborn for a bizarre purpose. Men used them as combat boot insoles or as top-tier bandages to stop bleeding from wounds. It was a terrifying irony that items designed to alleviate women's pain had been reduced to military supplies for the savage survival of males.

On the other hand, the glamorous cosmetics and beauty tools that used to carry a 'pink tax' were treated as trash to be kicked aside. The aesthetic value that once beautified the world had evaporated; only the calories to stave off starvation and destructive functionality became the sole currency of the new era.

Jason moved his feet toward the canned goods aisle. There, he saw a familiar back. It was Director Miller, Jason's former boss. Miller, who used to always boast a perfectly tailored suit and emphasize data efficiency, was now wearing a ragged shirt, sprawled on the floor, crying while clutching a single dented can of mackerel.

"Director... what are you doing here?"

Director Miller raised his head. His eyes were unfocused. "Jason... I don't know how to boil water. Without my wife... I don't remember how to turn on the stove, or how to wash rice. I worked my whole life to make money and gave it all to them, but why is this cold chunk of metal the only thing left in my hands?"

It was a massive cognitive dissonance. The men who prided themselves on their social status and economic power had become giant babies unable to take care of themselves once the 'care labor' that maintained daily life was castrated. The economic system lost its credit and regressed into the beastly barter of goods, and ironically, the first to collapse in it were the 'capable' men who occupied the upper echelons of society.

Jason walked past Director Miller and put a few cans of long-lasting Spam into his bag. On his way out, he looked down at the back of his hand again. He had gotten a shallow cut, perhaps from a shard of broken glass on the mart floor.

But it was strange. Blood should have been gushing from the wound, but only a tiny bit of dark red, oozing liquid seeped out. Even the pain was faint.

Jason tried to feel the heavy weight of the canned goods in his bag. But even that weight felt somewhat surreal. Amidst the rough shouts of the men filling the mart and the smell of rotting meat, Jason finally realized. It wasn't just the economy that had collapsed. The 'life' itself of all the males in this city, including himself, was slowly drying up from the roots.

Jason squeezed the cold tin can tightly, but he couldn't feel any warmth in his palm. A terrible emptiness, as if a ghost was stealing a ghost's food, crept up his ankles.
