The Colorless Forest, the End of the Balance Sheet

The red glare of the inferno burning at their backs faded slowly behind the dense ranks of ancient trees. The neo-humans' roars and the billionaires' death cries were swallowed by thick moss and fog.

The golden sheen of luxury goods that had symbolized civilization's vanity, the neon glow of stock charts that had danced across billionaires' monitors—all had turned to ash. What now stretched before David Sterling and Chloe Bennett was a forest stripped of every last trace of color, a perfect abyss ruled by nothing but cold grey fog and absolute blackness.

"Hff… hff…"

Chloe collapsed against the roots of a giant fern, gasping for breath. The air flooding her lungs was nothing like the acrid smoke of the compound. It was the dense, iron-tinged scent of leaf mold and fungi that had been rotting for decades—centuries, perhaps. The primordial smell of death and birth entwined beyond distinction.

David did not look back. He swept the surrounding darkness with keen, searching eyes.
A lifetime as a risk-assessment actuary had wired his brain to calculate variables and sketch standard deviations even in the most extreme circumstances. Inside Nathaniel's credit economy, inside the neo-humans' gear-driven system, that had been possible. Whether the adversary chased profit or pursued calories, there had always been a legible purpose.

But in this colorless abyss, the functions on the vast Excel sheet orbiting David's mind were throwing errors one by one, cascading into collapse.

Nature has no purpose. It simply exists. Here there was no interest to calculate, no cheap incentive to engineer into a strike. In the space left behind once every ledger-based logic of human control had been erased, David felt, for the first time in his life, the frigid terror of his own intellect rendered perfectly useless.

"David."
Chloe opened her mouth, wiping mud from her hazmat suit with trembling hands.

"When I was designing the bunker's mechanical room, the most important thing to me was predictability. Turn a valve and the pressure drops. Kill the motor and the noise stops. A world where cause and effect are under control. But here…"

Chloe looked up at the grotesque kudzu vines creeping through the darkness.
"This isn't a machine. It's a vast organism—radiation and wilderness randomly crossbreeding for ten years. The neo-humans we just fought were, in the end, remnants of the human world. But the true masters of this forest… they'll be beyond every biological assumption we've ever held."

She was right.
The capitalists' ledger and the neo-humans' mechanical gears had existed within rules made by humans. But this deep forest was a lawless zone that mocked every rule humanity had ever defined.

Rustle—

A faint, almost imperceptible friction reached them through the dense fog.
Both bodies locked rigid. David's hand shot out instinctively and clamped over Chloe's mouth.

It was not the sound of leaves brushing in the wind. Something massive and heavy was sliding across the damp earth. Utterly unlike the neo-humans' heavy combat-boot footfalls—an alien presence that raised gooseflesh to the bone.

David slowly turned his head toward the source of the sound.
In the grey-and-black darkness of the colorless forest, a bizarre phenomenon was unfolding. Along the carpet of moss and the surfaces of rotting tree stumps, an eerie blue-green bioluminescence had begun pulsing on and off, rhythmic as a heartbeat.

"What… is that?"
Chloe managed a strangled whisper through David's fingers.

The luminescence was spreading rapidly across the forest floor, flowing like living veins. An impossible, skin-crawling hue, sprouting over the colorless ruins of a dead city—something that could not belong to nature as they knew it. Inside David's skull, warning lights flared in a frenzy.

It was not a beautiful nocturnal vista. Like the lure of a deep-sea anglerfish, lit to draw prey close, this was a biological trigger—the forest's apex predator sensing the warmth of foreign organisms—humans—that had entered its territory.

At the terminus of the luminescent trail, barely a dozen meters from the two of them, within a dense thicket, two enormous yellow eyes bloomed open.

Not a whisper of breath. Those yellow orbs, flickering like thermal sensors, locked onto David and Chloe from a fixed elevation with absolute precision. For the eyes of a beast, the position was deformity-level high—nearly three meters above the ground, suspended in empty air.

"Don't move."
David whispered, his lips barely stirring.

Every risk they had faced until now—bankruptcy, overload—had been a terror reducible to numbers. But the unknown entity staring down at them from the darkness existed on no actuary's spreadsheet, on no engineer's blueprint. It was perfect, unquantifiable death itself.

Sssssk—CRRK.

The yellow-eyed entity slowly parted the undergrowth and stepped forward.
Illuminated by the faint glow of luminescent moss, its silhouette was a nightmare forged by radiation. Multiple joints, long and grotesquely bent like a spider's legs. Instead of fur, its massive body was sheathed in sharp, obsidian-like keratin plates. Through the gaps of its open maw, viscous fluid drooled in threads, dropping with soft, wet plinks.

The torment of the capstan gears that Preston and Cohen had endured, the terror of the explosion—all of it was nothing but a quaint human game compared to the overwhelming presence of this primordial wild.

Virtual credit creation, leverage, short squeeze. The grandiose vocabulary of capitalism that had ruled mankind offered no shield whatsoever before the gaping maw of this colossal predator. In the abyss of a forest where every balance sheet had burned to ash, David Sterling and Chloe Bennett were plummeting to the very bottom of the food chain—stripped down to raw meat.
