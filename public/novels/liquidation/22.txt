What the Bunker's Architect Saw at the Forest's Edge

In the pitch-black darkness, David Sterling and Chloe Bennett lay prone within a dense colony of giant ferns, studying the neo-human forward operating base in absolute silence.

Bathed in the pulsing of red light, the interior of the compound resembled the forge-floor of hell. But the neo-humans David observed were not mindless monsters stripped of reason. They moved with iron discipline. One group butchered the meat of hunted beasts; another ground bones into weapons; a third turned the turbine of a crude generator.

There was not a single wasted second in any of their actions. If the capitalism of the civilized age had run on documents and contracts, then this society ran on nothing but the laws of calories and thermodynamics—a perfect, merciless real economy.

"My God… It can't be."

A trembling gasp escaped Chloe as she swept the compound through the bunker's optical lens, which served as a makeshift telescope. Her gaze was fixed not on the bound billionaires but on the massive steel barriers encircling the compound's perimeter and the weapons the neo-humans wielded.

"David, look at the switchboard connected to that generator. And the plasma-cutter spears they're carrying."

Chloe's voice carried not mere terror but a deep, convulsive shock.

"Those aren't random chunks of scrap they scavenged from the wild. Those are… the specialized industrial-grade materials I requisitioned ten years ago, when I was designing this bunker, for the construction of its outer defensive perimeter."

David's brow furrowed. "What are you saying? Why would bunker materials be in their hands?"

Chloe pressed her palms against her face and shook her head.
"Ten years ago, when the apocalypse struck, one hundred billionaires sealed themselves inside the bunker. But the hundreds of subcontracted construction workers and junior engineers who had been conscripted to build that bunker were… abandoned outside. The billionaires locked the doors, unwilling to 'waste' the surplus food and oxygen it would cost to take them in. I can still hear the screams of the workers pounding on the steel doors, begging to be let in."

Chloe's eyes went cold.
"We assumed they all perished—starved and irradiated. But they didn't. The abandoned workers tore the construction equipment from the bunker's exterior and re-forged it into weapons. They gnawed on the flesh of beasts to survive and endured the radiation with nothing but their bare bodies. Over ten hellish years, they evolved into the most lethal predators in this forest."

A sinister chill slithered down David's spine.
It was perfect karmic justice. Ten years ago, the billionaires had discarded their spent workforce into the garbage heap of the wilderness to protect their surplus capital. But those workers had not died. They had shed the husk of civilization, armed themselves with overwhelming violence and survival instinct, and returned as true neo-humans—to fasten steel lassoes around the throats of the employers who had abandoned them.

"Ironic, isn't it. The capitalists trembling in there don't even realize they've been hunted by the very workers they laid off ten years ago."

David's gaze shifted to the massive H-beam column at the center of the compound.

There, the most grotesque and humiliating asset appraisal in human history was under way. The neo-humans had lined up the hundred captured billionaires and were forcibly tearing the fifty-thousand-dollar bespoke hazmat suits from their bodies.

"No! My clothes! My hazmat suit!"
Resort magnate Cohen wailed like an animal, but the neo-humans were merciless. In their eyes, the premium fabric embroidered with gold thread held no value whatsoever. All they wanted was the skin and the protein-rich muscle fiber hidden beneath the suit.

The stripped billionaires were hurled naked into the cold night air of the forest.
The bodies of those sleek elites who had once graced the cover of Forbes were pitiable. White skin, raised on nothing but cultured meat for ten years inside a bunker, hung loose and sagging. Dysentery and days of grueling labor had shriveled their muscles to pathetic husks.

The neo-human Alpha strode among the naked slabs of meat, conducting a quality inspection. He drove a steel skewer into the thigh of Preston Huntington III, former fourth-generation real-estate scion.

"AAAGH!"
Preston shrieked and thrashed in the mud. The Alpha checked Preston's meager muscle mass, clicked his tongue in disgust, and moved on.

Arthur Lindley, the former Chief Human Resources Officer, clung to the Alpha's legs, naked and shaking.
"P—please, spare me…! I know how to manage organizations! I can design a system to run your compound more efficiently—"

The Alpha did not pretend to listen. He seized Arthur by the nape of the neck and slammed him into the ground. There was no place here for Arthur's "survival contribution charter." To these beings, a human's value came in exactly two forms: a bio-battery to turn the turbine—slave—or a protein block to roast over tonight's charcoal—food.

With a single gesture from the Alpha, the neo-humans began sorting the billionaires into two groups. Those with reasonably thick frames and any stamina left were dragged toward the massive hand-cranked gear-turbine. Those like Cohen—old, sick, their muscle mass wasted away—were hauled toward the cold butcher's quarter where meat hooks dangled from the ceiling.

"No! Let go! Do you know who I am?! Do you have any idea how much money is in my Swiss accounts!"
Richard, a private-equity director being dragged toward the butcher's quarter, screamed through bloody tears, but that hollow incantation of capital conjured no miracle before the slaughterhouse of the wild.

A total inversion of value.
Billions in stock, doctoral degrees, dazzling rhetoric. Every abstract value that had adorned a human being in civilized society had been incinerated. In its place remained only the most savage metric: a few kilograms of protein and a handful of muscle fibers.

Chloe, fighting back nausea at the spectacle, gripped David's shoulder.
"What can we even do from here? There's no way to save them. Those neo-humans are perfect killing machines."

David pulled out his diary and, by touch alone in the darkness, scratched letters into the page.
"I have no intention of rescuing them. They're parasites of capitalism paying the invoice of annihilation—nothing more."

David's gaze moved past the billionaires' slaughterhouse to the dark corridor leading deeper into the compound—the passage where Julian "Jules" Cross had vanished moments ago, dragged away by the Alpha.

"Our objective is singular: sabotage the neo-humans' system and get out of this forest."

"Sabotage? How?"
Chloe asked, bewildered. "They don't control people with virtual debts like Nathaniel did. That place is a pure real economy—nothing but violence and calories. There isn't even a ledger to falsify. How do you destroy a system like that?"

A cold, surgical smile spread across David's lips.
"Chloe. There is no system in existence without a ledger. If Nathaniel's ledger was credit inscribed on paper, then the neo-humans' ledger is the electrical power their turbines produce. They require enormous amounts of electricity to sustain their overwhelming firepower, and to generate that electricity, they're grinding billionaires into bio-batteries."

David's eyes gleamed sharply toward the compound's interior.
"And a lunatic named Jules just crawled into the heart of that perfect engine. Jules will undoubtedly amplify the neo-humans' power grid twofold, threefold. The neo-humans will celebrate. But a system addicted to maximized efficiency exposes its most lethal vulnerability the moment it becomes dependent on that efficiency."

In his career, David had seen it hundreds of times: manufacturing firms that ratcheted conveyor-belt speed to its theoretical limit in pursuit of cost reduction and peak efficiency. In the short term, profits soared. But inevitably, a single overload event—one the overstressed machinery could no longer absorb—reduced the entire factory to ash.

"Tossing a single grain of sand into a perfectly spinning gear. That is how I handle risk."

David closed his diary and sank low into the underbrush.
"We need to find an infiltration route into the compound. Before Jules takes full control of their switchboard, we have to reach the system's circuit breaker first."

Chloe felt an unexpected surge of trust in David's cold gaze. In this forest, where everyone was devolving into beasts before the onslaught of physical violence, only this emotionless former actuary continued to analyze the world as a vast Excel spreadsheet—calculating lethal bugs with mechanical precision.

The two of them dissolved into the darkness and the deep shadows of the ferns, taking soundless steps toward the blood-red fortress awash in mechanical hum and human screams. The most precise and deadly act of systems hacking—launched from the very bottom of the food chain—was about to begin.
