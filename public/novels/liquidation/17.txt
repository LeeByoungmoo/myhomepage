17
The Bone-Grinding Dawn Labor of the Indebted

Four in the morning. While the sun still lay crushed beneath the ashen fog, what shattered the camp's stillness was the clash of metal and panting as bestial as a draft animal's.

"Get up! Preston! Get up and grab the axe!"

Cohen was kicking Preston Huntington III in the ribs as Preston lay curled outside Cohen's tent. Preston was yesterday's "blue-chip underlying asset"—the man who had torn his muscles hauling timber all day to claim an overwhelming first place. But of the liter of water he had earned, only a single mouthful had actually passed Preston's parched throat. The rest had been stripped away as contractually obligated dividends to Cohen and his other "investors."

Preston groaned, face pressed into the dirt.

"Please… just thirty more minutes… my muscles are tearing apart. I can't move my fingers…"

"Shut up! I invested every last one of my emergency food cubes in you because I believed you'd take first! If you don't claim the biggest tree before sunrise, you're delisted! If you don't go out and chop wood right now, I'll seize every last water voucher you have for breach of contract!"

Driven by Cohen's threats, Preston hauled himself up on trembling limbs. His hands were grotesque lumps of burst blisters fused with mud. The fourth-generation heir who had once commanded hundreds of servants from a Manhattan skyscraper was now a perfect slave-beast—forced to trudge through pre-dawn frost, bones grinding, to earn dividends for the shareholders who had invested medicine and food rations in his body.

Cohen was not alone. Throughout the camp, elderly and ailing billionaires who had reinvented themselves as "investors" were slapping their gladiators on the back and shoving them toward the forest.

David Sterling watched this nightmarish predawn commute from the shadows, unable to suppress the cold sneer spreading across his face.

Before the apocalypse, David had been assigned to assess the risk of a deal in which a Wall Street mega-private equity fund had acquired a solid Midwest manufacturing plant through leveraged buyout. The moment the fund took ownership, it transferred the astronomical debt it had incurred to purchase the factory directly onto the factory's own balance sheet. Overnight saddled with crushing liabilities, the factory had been forced to run its lines at a frenetic pace just to service the interest.

While the capitalists uncorked champagne at Manhattan's finest restaurants, toasting their "efficient turnaround," the Midwest factory workers were driven into murderous sixteen-hour rotating shifts. A fatigued worker's arm was severed by a conveyor belt. Others dropped dead from overwork and were carted away like refuse. But on the fund managers' Excel sheets, their blood and torn flesh were merely transmuted into the smooth figure labeled "labor cost optimization."

And now, ten years later, in this ruined forest, those same fund managers had become the "ground-down laborers" inside their own Excel sheets—crawling through the dirt on their hands and knees. The whip of debt and derivatives that capital had forged was now lashing their own flayed backs. What perfect, exquisite karmic justice.

"At this rate, a few of them will die of cardiac arrest from overwork before the day is out."

Chloe Bennett approached David's side, arms folded, and whispered as she watched Preston's staggering silhouette disappear into the trees. Hidden in the pocket of her hazmat suit was the animal bone—sliced clean through in a single stroke—that she had recovered from the forest's edge the day before.

"Are you going to tell them what we saw?"

At Chloe's question, David shook his head decisively.

"No. They wouldn't believe us. At best, they'd just package 'neo-human risk' as the underlying asset for yet another derivative and strangle each other over it. When this insane system Nathaniel has built collides with a genuine external shock, we only need to observe the sound it makes as it ruptures."

As David predicted, the billionaires had no inkling that an overwhelming predator lurked in the darkness beyond the trees, watching them. Consumed by a single hunger—to repay their debts and steal another man's water—they marched toward the forest.

Preston staggered deep into the fog-choked woods.

The nearest, most accessible trees had already been felled the day before. To meet his quota, he would have to venture into the hazard zone where beasts might prowl. His breathing tore at his lungs. The pain in his palms made it physically impossible to grip the rusted axe handle.

"Why… why am I doing this…"

Tears rolled down his blood-caked cheeks. During the bunker's ten years, Preston had proven his existence by wagering tens of millions in virtual chips on VR gambling tables. But the moment those substanceless virtual debts were downloaded into the physical world, his bones and muscles had been seized as collateral.

Through his unfocused eyes, Preston spotted a colossal dead tree. Thick enough for two men to wrap their arms around and still not meet. If he could roll that back to camp, first place was guaranteed—and he might finally spit water into the face of Cohen, the man who had been working him like a mule.

Preston let out a bestial howl, raised his axe, and charged the trunk.

CLANG—!

Instead of the heavy thud of blade biting bark, a bizarre, piercingly sharp crack rang through the forest. A bone-jarring recoil shot up Preston's arms. The rusted axe blade had shattered completely.

"Wh—what is this?"

Preston sank to the ground and stared up at the base of the dead tree. In the darkness, what he had failed to see was that the trunk's base was not wood at all. It was a massive steel frame, cunningly camouflaged with bark.

With trembling hands, Preston felt along the tree's surface. What met his fingertips was not the natural grain of wood, but the cold surface of an artificial structure—something welded and assembled with extreme precision. Through the gaps in the steel column, a faint blue light blinked.

This was no random ruin. Someone had intentionally installed it here—a product of chillingly advanced technology, designed to monitor or defend this forest corridor.

Ice-water terror cascaded down Preston's spine.

The bunker's billionaires were hacking at trees with decade-old military hatchets like cavemen. Meanwhile, somewhere in this forest, entities that had survived the apocalypse and evolved with vastly superior technology and firepower were breathing, watching, waiting.

Rustle—

Behind Preston, from the dense fog-shrouded undergrowth, came the sound of heavy footsteps. Too wide for a human stride. Too rhythmic—too chillingly regular—for an animal.

Preston could not even scream. He sucked in a sharp, silent breath. Slowly, he turned. In his dull, unfocused pupils, a single colossal, grotesque silhouette materialized through the fog—looming over him in absolute silence.

The "empire of credit and debt" that Nathaniel had created had reduced the billionaires to legal slaves. But nature and the evolution of the wild recognize no flimsy documents or ledgers. The true apex predator of the food chain was advancing on Preston—the weakest dregs of civilization—its intent to kill flickering in its eyes for the first time.
