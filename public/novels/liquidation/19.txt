The Joker's Intervention: A Broken Dispenser and the Collapse of Scarcity

The chaos unleashed by the cascading derivative defaults had driven the camp to the brink of total anarchy. Billionaires who had pledged their tents, their food, even the clothes on their backs as collateral were throttling each other and rolling through the mud, screaming for assets that had evaporated into thin air. The elegant elites who had once commanded the global economy were now indistinguishable from a pack of feral dogs, snarling and stripped of reason.

"Everyone, be silent! If you do not stop immediately, today's rations are cancelled in full!"

Nathaniel Pierce climbed onto a toppled concrete slab at the center of camp and roared. In one hand he clutched the critical valve he had extracted from the water purifier the day before, brandishing it like a royal scepter.

At the command of the man who held the physical lifeline—water—the billionaires' thrashing froze as though a switch had been thrown. Nathaniel looked down at the blood-soaked Preston Huntington III crumpled on the ground, contempt carved into every line of his face, and spoke.

"Have you forgotten the rules of the market? When the underlying asset—labor—goes bankrupt, it is only natural that the derivatives built upon it become worthless. The responsibility for a failed investment lies entirely with the investor."

"Then what about my debts! The debts I took on to invest in that bastard—what am I supposed to do about those!"
Cohen wailed, his face crusted with dried blood.

Nathaniel smiled without mercy.
"Bad debt must be written off. Effective today, Preston is permanently delisted from our camp's ledger. He is now a human being outside our system. And those of you who cannot service your own debts—starting tomorrow, you will cease playing investor and go into the forest yourselves to haul back double the firewood. This is restructuring."

Nathaniel was ruthless. He intended to dispose of the obsolete component called Preston and shackle the remaining debt-mired billionaires into a permanent slave caste. Exile from this camp meant death in the wild. Preston's eyes rolled back and he began laughing like a man who had lost his mind, while the other billionaires were so paralyzed with fear—knowing any of them could be discarded next—that they dared not even breathe.

David Sterling leaned against the rim of a trench, seeing through to the core of the power Nathaniel was wielding.

In David's memory surfaced the methods of the colossal cartel that had once monopolized the global diamond market. In truth, diamonds were not nearly as rare as people were led to believe. The cartel had hoarded enormous quantities in secret vaults, throttling supply to an extreme degree. Artificial scarcity. That was the sorcery that transmuted a lump of carbon—essentially a rock—into an eternal symbol of love worth millions of dollars.

Nathaniel's power operated on the same principle. That water purifier was a high-performance machine capable of filtering dozens of liters per hour. But by removing the valve and shutting it down, by rationing water one tantalizing drop at a time, Nathaniel had inflated the value of water to equal life itself. He who controls scarcity is God.

"Such a predictable repertoire, Nathaniel. Restructuring, monopoly, rationed supply. I'm yawning over here."

A mocking voice, accompanied by a bizarre screech of grinding metal, tore through the leaden silence.
Nathaniel's gaze whipped toward the source—Julian "Jules" Cross, founder of an aerospace empire.

Jules had pried open the lower panel of the dormant water purifier and was splicing together a high-performance battery pack he had smuggled out of the bunker with the motor components from a dismantled drone, lashing them into a grotesque web of wiring.

"Jules, get your hands off that machine this instant. Touching my assets—"

"Your assets? Don't make me laugh."
Jules spat a wire from between his teeth and cackled.

"You tricked everyone into thinking water was valuable because it was scarce. Clutching that one little valve made you feel like the king of this forest, didn't it? But I'm about to prove just how pathetic your rinky-dink command economy really is. The most perfect way to destroy capitalism isn't through shortage—it's through such obscene abundance that the whole thing drowns!"

Jules force-shorted the drone motor's power switch.

WHIIIIIIIRR—BANG!

An explosive roar of motor revolutions erupted from inside the water purifier. The empty socket where Nathaniel had extracted the pressure valve was irrelevant. Jules had hard-wired the drone's high-output motor directly to the purifier's direct-feed pump, obliterating the filter's safety limiter entirely.

"No—!"
Nathaniel screamed and lunged, but it was too late.

KRAAAAAAASH—!!!

A monstrous jet of water erupted from the purifier's outlet like a fire hose. This was not the pathetic trickle that had sustained life drop by precious drop. The machine howled as it vacuumed muddy water from the surrounding puddles, and through its internal nano-filters, perfectly transparent, ice-cold ultra-pure water cascaded through the air like a waterfall.

The torrent hammered into the mud floor, instantly carving a massive pool at the center of camp.

"Water… It's water!"
"It's actually clean water!"

The eyes of the severely dehydrated billionaires went white.
They forgot Nathaniel's authority, forgot Preston's bankruptcy, forgot their own debts—and charged at the gushing column of water like animals.

"Don't push! Move!"
Dozens of billionaires dove into the pool. They dropped prone into the filthy mud and lapped at the water, panting, scooping it up with burst-blistered palms and pouring it down their throats in a frenzy. Custom bespoke hazmat suits were soaked through with mud, but no one cared. They were a herd of buffalo discovering an oasis in a parched desert—growling and gulping with bottomless greed.

"Stop the machine! You lunatic! I said stop it!"

Nathaniel, unable to withstand the water pressure and drenched from head to toe, seized Jules by the collar. His immaculately styled hair clung to his face like strands of seaweed, and his once-elegant features were grotesquely contorted with terror and rage.

Jules, thoroughly soaked, erupted into the laughter of a madman.
"Stop it? How do you plug a dam that's already burst! Look, Nathaniel! The capital you were so desperate to control is rolling around in sewage on the ground! When water is this absurdly common, what good is that precious little 'debt ledger' of yours!"

It was the perfect annihilation of value.
An asset stripped of scarcity degrades to garbage. The billionaires drank until their stomachs distended and wept tears of euphoria in the mud. The instant water became infinite, the leash Nathaniel had fastened around their necks—the "survival contribution score"—crumbled like a sandcastle. There was no longer any need to risk one's life felling trees for water, to trade derivatives, or to kneel before another man.

David stood in the spray of the gushing water column, recording this surreal spectacle in his diary.

If the counterfeit currency Jules had scattered the day before had been financial inflation, then this ruptured machine was real-economy inflation. Nathaniel's power had been a mirage erected upon paper-scrap ledgers and manufactured scarcity. A single act of the Joker's deranged sabotage, and the miniature capitalism erected by the top 0.001 percent of the human elite had been annihilated in a mere four days—physically and systemically, with perfect totality.

"Quite the spectacle. A complete redistribution of wealth."
Chloe Bennett, standing at David's side, murmured coldly.

The billionaires lay in the pool clutching their swollen bellies, laughing. Cohen spat water into the air and cheered. Even Preston—who had been driven mad by the terror of bankruptcy—had shoved his face into the edge of the puddle and was gulping water. Nathaniel sat slumped beside the ruined machine, watching the evaporation of his power with hollow, devastated eyes.

But buried beneath the roar of cascading water and the billionaires' frenzied cheering, not one of them noticed the change in the forest.

"David."
Chloe's voice dropped—low and razor-edged, like a bowstring drawn to breaking. Her eyes were fixed beyond the water column, staring into the pitch-black tree line where dense fog pooled.

"I think the party's over."

David's gaze followed Chloe's toward the forest.
Between the grotesque roar of the purifier's motor, the sound of undergrowth rustling in unison reached his ears. This was not the noise of one or two animals. Through the fog, massive silhouettes were rippling forward, compressing around the camp in a perfect semicircular encirclement.

Shhhhk—
The first thing to pierce the fog was a plasma spear-tip, sparking blue. Then the neo-humans themselves materialized—towering two-meter giants clad in grotesque patchwork armor of raw animal hide and scrap metal—seeping soundlessly into the camp's perimeter like a wolf pack herding its prey.

At the precise moment Nathaniel's virtual rules shattered beyond repair, the true masters of nature raised their blades of execution over one hundred fat, defenseless prey gorging themselves on water. The blood-red curtain of the brutal third act was rising.
