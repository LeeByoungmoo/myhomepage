21
The Uncontrollable Casting Vote

The captured prey were roped in single file and dragged toward the lightless depths of the forest.

The neo-humans' method of binding was savagely efficient. They had twisted animal sinew and thin steel wire into lasso loops, which they fastened around the billionaires' necks one after another. If the man in front stumbled and fell, the wire cinched tighter around the man behind. The custom bespoke boots that had once ascended the airstairs of private jets in New York now carved wretched furrows through mud and animal excrement.

In the darkness, the only sounds that escaped were suppressed groans and the shuffle of feet. At the slightest resistance or faltered step, the neo-humans would—without so much as turning around—bring a steel shaft crashing down on kneecaps or shoulders with zero mercy. Nathaniel Pierce, caked in blood and filth, was lashed somewhere near the middle of the line, walking with bloodshot eyes fixed on empty space. The empire of ledgers and credit he had created was rubble, and all that remained was the sensation of cold steel wire biting into his throat.

But within this hellish march, a single perfectly incongruous noise was scraping against the silence of the forest.

"Hey, big guy! What's the tensile strength on this wire? Judging by the smoothness, it looks like you unspooled elevator cables from some derelict building and re-smelted them—where'd you find the furnace? Or did you evolve the ability to secrete molten iron from your bodies?"

Julian "Jules" Cross.
Even as the lasso around his neck dragged him forward, Jules's eyes were bright with the spark of a billionaire eccentric who had just boarded a cutting-edge roller coaster, chattering without pause.

One of the neo-humans, apparently fed up with the noise, drove a saw-toothed club into Jules's abdomen.
"Gkh!"
Jules spat blood-laced saliva and tumbled to the ground. Any other billionaire would have fainted or dissolved into tears from that blow. But Jules, face contorted in pain, erupted into a grotesque peal of laughter.

"Ha ha ha! Incredible impact! Perfect physics engine! That muscle-fiber density of yours has to be at least three times that of a normal human—radiation mutation or genetic modification?"

*Madman.* Preston Huntington III, watching from behind, trembled with terror and cursed Jules silently.

David Sterling and Chloe Bennett were shadowing the procession at a cautious distance through the pitch-black darkness, holding their breath. Watching Jules—delirious with excitement even as he was dragged along like an animal—David recalled the uncontrollable variable he had encountered in the age of civilization before the end.

Fifteen years earlier, David's insurance firm had underwritten an astronomical disaster-insurance policy on a satellite-launch project for the aerospace company Jules had founded. On launch day, the rocket—ascending on a perfectly calculated trajectory—had suddenly lost control and detonated in the atmosphere, vaporizing billions of dollars into the sky.

When David visited Jules's headquarters to investigate the cause of the accident, he found Jules watching a 3D holographic replay of the exploding rocket's debris trajectories—eating popcorn.

"What was the cause? The system was flawless."
To David's cold interrogation, Jules had answered with manic eyes.
"I tweaked the code a little. A perfect success is just too boring. I simply wanted to see with my own eyes what kind of beautiful entropy emerges when a hundred million components collide at a margin of error of 0.001 seconds in outer space. Money? You can always print more numbers."

Capitalism is a system that operates strictly on the premise of rational self-interest. It can only be controlled when participants pursue profit and fear loss. But to Jules, money, power, even his own life held no meaning whatsoever. To him, the world was merely a vast simulation game—throw in a variable, observe what grotesque output it produces.

When he had cast his sadistic vote in favor of the brutal rules that buried Preston in debt inside the bunker, when he had counterfeited Nathaniel's water vouchers to trigger hyperinflation and blown up the purifier the day before—Jules's motive had been singular each time: *Which option produces the more entertaining catastrophe?*

"If Jules provokes them, everyone dies."
Chloe whispered low and tugged at David's sleeve.

David shook his head.
"On the contrary, his insanity is buying us time. Those neo-humans believe they hold the leash right now, but in truth they've just dragged the most unpredictable time bomb in existence into their own heart."

The deeper the procession plunged into the forest, the more grotesquely the air transformed around them. Through the damp moss-smell, the dense tang of machine oil and the iron reek of blood pricked at their nostrils.

Before long, the vanguard of the neo-human column halted. The dragged billionaires, gasping for breath, lifted their heads. In their dull eyes, the neo-humans' massive forward operating base revealed itself in all its overwhelming scale—a monstrous hybrid of raw nature and scrap metal.

This was no cave of primitives. It was a fortress, built upon the skeletal frame of what had once been a military installation or industrial complex, cunningly camouflaged with colossal ferns and creeping vines. Crude generators—cobbled together from roughly torn solar panels—churned with a grinding din throughout the compound. Under a blood-tinged red glow, dozens of neo-humans were maintaining weapons or butchering unidentifiable meat.

"Dear God…"
Cohen collapsed and urinated in his trousers. Not all of the massive hides hanging from the butchering racks belonged to animals.

The neo-humans fastened the steel collars of the captured billionaires, one by one, to a towering H-beam steel column in the center of the compound. The billionaires, skin abraded and bleeding, clung to each other's bodies and quaked like livestock awaiting the slaughterhouse.

Then, from atop a derelict shipping container perched at the highest point of the fortress, came the thud of massive footsteps.

An overwhelming physique—easily two and a half meters tall. Over jet-black mechanical armor, animal skulls dangled as ornamentation. This was the unmistakable Alpha of the pack. When the Alpha leapt down from the container, the ground trembled with a metallic boom.

The Alpha surveyed the hundred frail prey tethered to the steel column with something resembling curiosity.

"H—hey! Can you hear me!"
Suddenly Nathaniel Pierce forced his trembling legs upright and shouted at the Alpha. The other billionaires, paralyzed with terror, stared at Nathaniel in disbelief.

Nathaniel had not yet surrendered. Being dragged here by the beasts of the forest was a nightmare, but the instant he laid eyes on this compound, the capitalist instinct buried in his marrow had stirred once more. *If they have generators and a hierarchy, then this is ultimately a system. Where there is a system, negotiation and control are always possible.*

"You appear to be their leader. We are useful personnel. Among us are world-class engineers and men with medical expertise. Rather than simply boiling us down for broth, keeping us alive and exploiting our labor would deliver far greater profit to your operation. Allow me to offer a binding subordination contract, with their labor as collateral—"

CRACK—!

Before Nathaniel could finish, a colossal steel gauntlet shot from behind the Alpha and backhanded Nathaniel across the face.

"GRAAAH!"
With the crunch of shattering jawbone, Nathaniel was launched through the air and slammed into the steel column. Teeth spilled from his mouth like kernels of corn. The petty, three-inch tongue that had controlled others through documents and pens his entire life was, before overwhelming physical violence, less than the whine of a mosquito.

The Alpha ignored the crumpled Nathaniel and began inspecting the muscle condition of each billionaire chained to the column. The neo-humans had no interest whatsoever in the billionaires' knowledge or technical expertise. Their fingers jabbed at calves and thighs, measuring only the mass of protein and the durability of muscle fiber available for labor.

At the end of this grotesque quality-assurance inspection, the Alpha's gaze came to rest on Julian Cross, who was giggling where he was bound.

"Hurts, doesn't it, Nathaniel? What did I tell you! Ledgers from the old world don't work anymore!"
Jules was mocking the blood-drenched Nathaniel, swaying his body as though about to break into a dance.

The Alpha stepped in front of Jules. From behind a mask fashioned of animal bone, a breath threaded with a cold mechanical rasp hissed outward. Even in the shadow of death, Jules showed no trace of fear, craning his neck up with defiant rigidity.

"Hello, chief. Your base—the layout's an absolute mess, you know that? You've got the solar-panel inverters wired in series only, so your power-loss ratio is over forty percent. Unshackle me and give me a few lengths of wire, and I can double the efficiency of that scrap heap. How about it—deal?"

It was lunacy. A madman critiquing the power efficiency of a compound's generator on the killing floor.
But astonishingly, the Alpha hesitated.

The Alpha reached out with an enormous hand and gripped the steel wire looped around Jules's neck. The billionaires squeezed their eyes shut, certain the Alpha would snap Jules's neck. But instead, the beast's fist yanked the end of the leash sharply, tore Jules free from the column, and began dragging him roughly toward a dark corridor deeper inside the compound.

"Oho! VIP treatment? I like where this is going!"
Without so much as a struggle, Jules cackled and vanished into the monsters' lair.

From far away, hidden behind the rubble of a derelict building at the compound's perimeter, David watched it all unfold—and felt a chill crawl down his spine.

Those neo-humans were not savages. They sneered at Nathaniel's hollow credit economy and his paper contracts, but they could see with perfect clarity the value of essential engineering—of tangible, physical knowledge that served survival. Even as they reduced the most enfeebled ruling class to food and slaves, they had recognized at first glance the worth of Jules—the "bug" capable of evolving their own system.

"If that lunatic gives wings of technology to these apex predators… this forest will become a perfect hell."

Chloe murmured, her face drained of color.

David did not answer. He looked up at the night sky.
The farce of Acts One and Two—where the billionaires had exploited others by leaning on virtual wealth—was finished. The moment Jules's casting vote merged with the neo-humans' brute force, the men who had once stood at the pinnacle of capitalism would be strapped to the operating table of Act Three: the most savage form of real economy in human history, reduced to perfect biological components.
