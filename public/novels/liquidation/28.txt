The Perfect Spreadsheet That Eliminated Humans

The silence that settled over the colossal logistics warehouse made it feel like a tomb of metal.

The blood-soaked stench of the savagery raging above was perfectly sealed out. Only the cool scent of refrigerant and ozone and corroded steel lingered at the tips of their noses. The blue glow pouring from the control room's massive screen illuminated the pallid faces of David Sterling and Chloe Bennett, draining them of every trace of color.

Chloe drifted toward the dust-blanketed console as if in a trance.
"Incredible. External power must have been completely severed, yet it's maintained standby mode for ten years on nothing but the underground geothermal generator. With battery efficiency and an algorithm this advanced… this is the apex AI model Silicon Valley achieved on the eve of annihilation."

Chloe brushed a decade's worth of dust from the console's physical keyboard and tapped several keys. The pixels on the screen realigned with a grotesque crackle.

[System Standby. Order Processing: 0. Awaiting Input.]

David read the cold sentence with arid detachment.
This was the crystallization of hyper-automation—the force that had thrown millions of workers onto the streets during the age of civilization. The ultimate utopia the capitalists had engineered to erase, from the ledger entirely, the uncertain variables of human sweat and blood, strikes and wage negotiations. Within this system, no one was hurt and no one complained. It was a perfect Excel sheet in which nothing existed but commands and processing, input and output.

"Chloe, can we control this system? We need access to a bypass exit to the surface, or to this facility's defensive weapons systems."

Chloe connected her tablet—brought from the bunker—to the console's main port.
"I'll try. The firewall's still up, but if the security protocol hasn't been updated in ten years, my engineering master key might be able to break through—"

BEEP—!

Before Chloe could finish, a shrill alarm blared through the entire control room. The enormous screen flashed a sinister red and vomited new text.

[WARNING: Unregistered Biological Entities detected.]
[Scan complete. Asset Code: Unidentifiable. Inventory Classification: Surplus Organic Material—Marked for Disposal.]

A cold, mirthless smile crossed David's lips.
"There you have it. To the ultimate system that capitalism created, we human beings are no longer the creators or the controllers. We are merely unidentifiable garbage—an Error—cluttering the clean cells of a pristine spreadsheet."

"This is not the time for smiling, David!"
Chloe hammered at the keyboard.

WHIIIIIRR—CLANK!

Hundreds of yellow robotic arms suspended from the cavernous ceiling pivoted in unison with a bone-snapping mechanical screech. Each arm, tipped with a red laser sensor like an insect's antenna, began locking focus on the control room where David and Chloe stood.

This space had never been designed for human beings. The logistics center's robots were programmed to eliminate every physical variable that obstructed the movement of packages. The minimal safety protocols that once safeguarded human life—Asimov's Laws, if they had ever been coded—had long since been deleted at the programming stage, sacrificed to cost reduction and efficiency maximization.

"I'm entering a Manual Override code, but the firewall's too thick! Without the old-world security code that carries top-level admin privileges, I can't stop these things!"
Chloe shouted, cold sweat running down her face beneath the red glow.

Dozens of massive robotic arms, extending from the ceiling like serpents, were writhing their way toward the control room's glass wall. At their tips, instead of suction pads for gripping boxes, titanium pincers capable of crushing animal bone and industrial barcode scanners glinted menacingly.

David studied the code on the screen with perfect composure.
"Top-level admin privileges…"

David opened the drawer of memory from twelve years ago, when he had conducted a risk audit of this very mega-scale automated logistics warehouse to underwrite its fire insurance and liability policy. The CEO of the tech company that had delivered the system had boasted to David:
*"This system is perfect. Only a single failsafe code can switch these machines from production mode to destruction mode. It's a poison pill—designed to shut the entire factory down in the event of a hostile takeover."*

"Step aside, Chloe."

David approached the console and began typing without hesitation.
What he entered was not a complex engineer's hack. It was a financial term—and an absolute self-destruct command—that the titans of Wall Street had hardcoded as a defense against hostile M&A before the end of the world.

[System Command: Liquidation_Protocol_Chapter_11]

Chapter 11. Bankruptcy protection filing and asset liquidation protocol.
When a company defaults, this was capitalism's final ledger-clearing button—the one that force-dumps or shreds all surplus inventory at fire-sale prices.

The instant David struck the worn Enter key, the robotic arms—inches from smashing through the control room's glass wall—froze in midair as if by magic.

The red warning on the screen vanished. In its place, cold green text cascaded line by line.

[Command accepted. Initiating Bankruptcy and Asset Liquidation Protocol.]
[Currently registered valid assets in warehouse: 0.]
[Commencing intake of external surplus inventory. Switching to disposal procedures.]

"David… what have you done?"
Chloe stared at the robotic arms frozen in the air, her voice trembling.

"I've made these machines recognize us not as targets for elimination, but as the bankruptcy trustees executing the liquidation proceedings."
David absently straightened the collar of his tie-less shirt, his tone bone-dry.

KRRRRRUMBLE—!

At that moment, a massive tremor rippled from the far end of the warehouse.
It was the sound of the enormous cargo-elevator hatch—sealed for ten years—connecting to the surface, grinding open. In response to the "intake of external surplus inventory" command David had just entered, the system had unsealed the surface entrance to vacuum everything above down into the warehouse for disposal.

"You opened the surface hatch?! Are you insane? Up there are neo-humans driven berserk by the explosion, and those radiation monsters that were hunting us!"
Chloe screamed in horror.

"I know."
David watched the system's operational status on the screen, expressionless.

The moment the surface hatch opened, grey fog poured through the ceiling alongside the roars of beasts and the panicked shouts of neo-humans who had survived the explosion. The hideous predators that had been prowling near the hatch, stalking prey, were being sucked helplessly into the suddenly gaping hole in the earth.

WHIIIIIIIRR—!

The underground fulfillment center's system awoke in full.
Across tens of thousands of racks, the thousands of unmanned forklifts and steel robotic arms that had slumbered like the dead opened their eyes in unison. In the system's lenses, the surface predators were not terrifying monsters or armed neo-humans. They were simply unscanned merchandise without barcodes—bad inventory to be fed immediately into the shredder for liquidation.

"Aren't you curious what kind of balance sheet emerges when the most horrifying machines capitalism ever built collide with the most horrifying monsters nature ever evolved?"

David stared through the control room's reinforced glass at the predators cascading from the surface.
The instant the massive obsidian creature—the one that had hungered for human blood and flesh—plummeted underground and roared, hundreds of steel robotic arms, devoid of a single gram of emotion, swarmed down upon it like a hive of hornets—to sort it for disposal.

Into the cold gears of the most advanced unmanned system ever built, the uncontrollable wild was being drawn. The stage of a perfect liquidation—where blood and steel would collide—raised its merciless curtain in the abyss deep underground.
