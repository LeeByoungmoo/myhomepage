Humans Classified as VIP Assets

The red countdown timer on the ceiling pointed at the dwindling minutes, draining away at a pace that dried the blood.

The control-room floor had already heated to a temperature that turned rubber soles sticky and viscous. Beyond the reinforced glass, the half-bisected radiation creature was flailing in its death throes, spraying acid, while unmanned forklifts bulldozed its torn flesh into the maw of the colossal shredder. Bodily fluid and sparks from severed wiring tangled in the air; the vast logistics warehouse had become, in the most literal sense, a cybernetic hell.

"How are we supposed to get through that! We'll be shredded within ten seconds of stepping outside!"
Chloe Bennett screamed in despair.

But David Sterling was not watching the carnage beyond the window. He was tearing through the cabinets and drawers inside the control room.

"A capitalist system is merciless to external threats, but endlessly generous to the assets registered in its own ledger. There is only one way to cross that killing-floor conveyor belt: make these insane machines recognize us not as garbage to be shredded, but as the most precious VIP freight to be transported with absolute care."

David's hand drove the pipe wrench into the lock of a titanium safe in the corner of the control room—a cabinet reserved for executive emergency supplies. After several blows the safe door tore free, and from inside, a dust-laden roll of foil stickers tumbled out.

These were not the paper barcodes affixed to ordinary shipping boxes. They were Priority A-Class RFID barcode stickers—the kind attached, before the apocalypse, only to board-level executives inspecting the logistics center or to the highest-value critical assets.

"Stick this on your body."
David peeled off two barcode stickers and handed one to Chloe.

"The system doesn't read visual shape—only the serial number on this sticker. The moment we walk through that door with these barcodes on our foreheads or chests, we will cease to be human. We will be classified as the most expensive, most valuable freight in this warehouse."

Chloe peeled the backing from her sticker with trembling hands and pressed it to the chest of her hazmat suit. David pressed his firmly onto the center of his own forehead.

The timer ticked down.
Cracks were beginning to spider across the control room's glass, driven by the heat radiating from below.

"We go."
David pulled the manual-release lever on the control-room door.

HISSSS—CLANG!

The instant the door opened, a murderous wave of heat and the stench of blood crashed inward like a storm. The two gasped for air and sprinted onto the killing floor of the main sorting zone.

The moment they emerged, three massive insectoid robotic arms—mid-dismemberment of a neo-human corpse nearby—swiveled toward them with an immediate mechanical whine. Titanium pincers shot toward their necks like striking serpents.

"AAAAAAH!"
Chloe squeezed her eyes shut and screamed.

But a fraction of a second before the pincers could bite into flesh, the red laser scanners mounted on each robotic arm swept across the barcode on David's forehead and the barcode on Chloe's chest.

BRRRING—!

The murderous steel arms froze in midair as if by sorcery.
The indicator lights on the robots' sensor lenses shifted—from the red of warning to the soft blue of stability and deference.

[Barcode recognized. Asset Class: Board-Level VIP (Priority A-Class).]
[Return to Sender protocol activated. Routing freight to Executive Elevator.]

Instead of tearing the two apart, the robotic arms minimized the pressure of their titanium pincers to the gentlest possible setting and cradled David's and Chloe's waists—as though handling a priceless crystal ornament.

"Hff—!"
The two were lifted into the air, suspended from the robotic arms. The machines set them down with exquisite care onto the high-speed priority conveyor belt running along the topmost level of the logistics center.

WHIIIIIRR—
The conveyor belt engaged at high speed, carrying the two smoothly toward the deepest recess of the facility—toward the executive elevator.

David lay on the speeding belt and looked down at the grotesque liquidation unfolding beneath them.

On the adjacent rail, the obsidian creature's tail was being severed by an industrial chainsaw, spraying green fluid like a fountain. On the level below, the half-incinerated torsos of neo-humans were being classified as defective freight and dropping endlessly into the smelter. The predators' hideous death cries shook the warehouse, but around David and Chloe—registered as VIP freight—dozens of escort robots flew in protective formation, preventing any hazard from approaching.

In the dead center of hell, where the masters of the wild were being ground to nothing in the machine's gears, two human beings with barcodes stuck to their foreheads were gliding through on the most secure, most comfortable first-class treatment on Earth. What a spine-chillingly perfect black comedy of capitalism.

"We're really… we're actually being treated as cargo…"
Chloe let out a hollow, disbelieving laugh.

David ran a finger across the barcode on his forehead and answered dryly.
"Human dignity has no value in the marketplace. The reason we survived is not because we possess hearts and brains. It is because we were wearing a high-value number the system could read."

The timer now pointed at its final minutes.
The exhaust vents along the warehouse's lower sections sealed shut in unison, and ultra-high-temperature gas for sterilization incineration began flooding into the facility. The air warped like a heat mirage, and every breath sent a searing agony through their lungs.

CLANK—

At last the conveyor belt reached its terminus, and a single robotic arm gently lifted the two and set them down before an enormous titanium door.

[Executive Elevator. Routing to Safe Zone.]

Chloe slammed the elevator's call button.
Independent power drawn from the underground geothermal generator groaned with a heavy metallic sound and parted the titanium doors. The two hurled themselves inside.

"Close the door! Close it now!"
David hammered the close button on the elevator panel in a frenzy.

The timer raced toward zero.
Through the narrowing gap of the closing titanium doors, the sound of the warehouse's massive incinerator valves opening fully thundered upward.

[00:00. Executing Incineration Protocol.]

KRAAAAAAAAAASH—!!!

As though the surface of the sun had detonated, merciless red flames at eight hundred degrees Celsius consumed the entire tens-of-thousands-of-square-meter logistics warehouse in an instant.

Steel melted. The flesh of radiation creatures and neo-humans vaporized to white ash in under a second. A blinding tidal wave of light—in which machines and monsters, the garbage of capitalism and the mutations of evolution were incinerated into a perfectly equal state of zero—surged toward the elevator.

SLAM!

The titanium doors sealed shut in a perfect join, severing the tongue of flame.
The elevator's interior was swallowed by a breathless silence. Through the thick firewall, the only sensation that reached them was the deep, bass tremor of everything below being consumed—felt through the soles of their feet.

WHIIIIIRR—
The executive elevator emitted a smooth hum and began its vertical ascent, leaving the burning underground hell behind, climbing toward the impossibly distant surface.

Chloe sank to the floor, hugging her knees, shaking.
David leaned against the wall, steadying his ragged breathing. The VIP barcode sticker on his forehead, soaked with sweat, peeled loose and dropped to the floor.

One hundred billionaires who had enslaved humans with virtual debt.
Neo-humans who had tried to slaughter them with overwhelming violence and a real economy of flesh.
And the automation algorithm that had classified all of that organic matter as bad inventory and incinerated it to perfection.

The insane balance-sheet war that had raged across the wild forest and the underground logistics center was now perfectly liquidated within the subterranean flames—without a single record left behind.

David reached into his coat with a bloodstained hand and drew out his sole remaining inheritance: a leather diary.
The elevator's floor-indicator was climbing rapidly, approaching the top level of the bunker—the place where they had first set foot ten years ago.

"Chloe."
David looked up at the floor indicator and spoke, his voice cold.

"Do you know what waits after a Chapter 11 bankruptcy is complete?"

Chloe raised her pale face.

"Once every debt and every residue of the past has been incinerated… a new owner always appears—ready to claim the clean ledger."

DING—!

The elevator arrived at the top floor with a clear chime.
Before the two survivors who had returned from hell, the titanium doors began to open, slowly. On the ashes of a world where every rule had been incinerated, the stage awaited—ready for the new architect who would claim that blank ledger.
