The Survival Contribution Manual, Written in Blood

At the base of a towering dead tree at camp center, three strips of carefully peeled birch bark had been pinned with sharpened bone shards. These were the "Survival Contribution Guide," which Arthur Lindley—the former Chief Human Resources Officer—had spent the entire night inscribing in dense charcoal script.

It was a devil's statute—the employee handbook of a civilized-era mega-corporation grotesquely warped to fit the ecosystem of an annihilated forest.

Article 1: The value of labor shall be measured solely by its output (the dryness of firewood, the caloric value of hunted food). The suffering of the process is excluded from evaluation.
Article 2: Sick leave to defer debt repayment shall not be recognized. Physical illness shall be deemed a failure of personal risk management.
Article 3: Any individual who steals camp assets (Water Vouchers, the purification filter) or damages the system shall render "physical compensation" for the violation of private property rights.

"Read carefully, all of you. The era when ignorance served as absolution is over. From today, this guide is the absolute constitution of our camp."

When Arthur's recitation ended, a heavy silence hung among the trembling billionaires. They were beginning to understand, with marrow-deep clarity, that they had been reduced to expendable components who must risk their lives for Nathaniel's water. But they did not yet grasp how much real blood that constitution would demand.

That truth was demonstrated, in horrifying fashion, later that afternoon.

"Give it back! That's my voucher!"

A rough scream tore through the camp's perimeter. Richard—a former private equity executive who had been bedridden with a back injury sustained during firewood collection—had been caught rifling through a sleeping neighbor's belongings, stealing a single Water Voucher.

Richard clamped the stolen slip of paper between his teeth and sprinted wildly toward the purifier.

"Water! Give me water! I have a voucher! Turn on the filter!"

But before his hand could reach the machine, Preston Huntington III—club in hand—drove a vicious kick into the back of Richard's knee.

"Gkh—!"

Richard crashed face-first into the mud. Preston planted a boot on the fallen man's back and pried the crumpled voucher from his mouth.

"You miserable rat. Stealing someone's private property?"

Preston's eyes were bloodshot. Having been reduced to Nathaniel's first slave and attack dog, Preston was becoming addicted to a perverse thrill—a twisted sense of superiority derived from crushing men who had once been his equals.

Nathaniel and Arthur arrived, drawn by the commotion. Nathaniel delicately wiped the crumpled voucher with a handkerchief and clicked his tongue.

"Richard, I'm sorry. Under Article 3 of the Survival Contribution Guide, your theft constitutes an unpardonable serious offense. Since you have no assets from which to levy a fine, we'll need to exact 'physical compensation.'"

"Ph—physical compensation? When I was running my private equity fund, do you know how much capital I channeled into your fund? Nathaniel, please, just look the other way this once. I was going insane with thirst!"

Richard pleaded through tears and snot.

But Nathaniel shook his head with graceful finality.

"If I defer enforcement for the sake of old friendships, market confidence collapses. Arthur, proceed."

Arthur nodded coldly and jutted his chin toward Preston.

"Representative Preston. Strike the thief's wrist with your club. Ensure he never touches another person's property again."

Every person in the forest stopped breathing.

Preston, too, flinched and looked down at the club in his hand. Not long ago, he and Richard had laughed together over stocks at an exclusive wine bar. Now he was being ordered to shatter the man's bones.

"You want me… to do it myself?"

Preston's voice wavered.

Nathaniel whispered, low and soft.

"Preston, you are the guardian of my system. If you do not punish this man, tomorrow someone will tear open your tent and steal your water ration. Violence is an essential cost of maintaining order."

Something grotesque shifted behind Preston's eyes. He began projecting the crushing weight of his own debt and the shame of his fall onto the man prostrate before him. "I'm living in this misery, and you dare break the rules to steal water for free?"

Preston let out a bestial scream and raised the club high.

CRACK—!

The dull impact and the sound of splintering bone echoed across the ruins.

"AAAAAAGHHH!!!"

Richard's wrist bent at a sickening angle, and blood fountained upward. He writhed in agony, rolling through the mud, but Preston did not stop. Like a man unhinged, he brought the club down again and again on Richard's shoulders and back. Across Preston's white hazmat suit, the red blood of a former colleague splattered upward.

David Sterling sat on a fallen log and watched the bloodshed with clinical detachment.

Capital's dominion is not completed when the capitalist himself takes up the blade. Perfect control is achieved when the most subjugated class voluntarily tramples those even weaker, defending the capitalist's system of their own free will.

In the financial empire where David had once worked, the executioner's dance of layoffs was always performed not by the chairman, but by middle managers who had risen from the labor ranks. Intoxicated by the relief of not being fired themselves, and drunk on the meager authority granted from above, they had severed their colleagues' livelihoods. Nathaniel, without a single drop of blood on his own hands, had forged Preston into capitalism's perfect monster.

Only after Richard lost consciousness did Preston's beating stop.

Preston stood clutching the bloodied club, drawing ragged breaths. His face was streaked with blood and mud, but his eyes glinted with the grotesque elation of having exercised power. The remaining billionaires were utterly crushed by this display of terror, bowing their heads in mouse-silent submission.

"Excellent. Order has been restored."

Nathaniel smiled with satisfaction, stepping delicately around the blood-soaked ground.

With that, Nathaniel's currency (the Water Vouchers) and Arthur's law (the Guide) had fed on real blood and ascended to absolute authority. No one dared question or resist the system. A perfect dictatorship—complete.

At least, until the mad joker stepped out of his tent.

"Wow, what a moving display of judicial justice! I'm practically in tears."

Into the petrified center of camp strode Julian "Jules" Cross, applauding with theatrical exaggeration. In both hands, he carried the document briefcase that had been issued to each resident in the bunker.

Nathaniel's brow creased imperceptibly. Jules was always the one variable that defied control.

"Jules. The mood is rather unpleasant for sharing commentary. Go back to your tent."

Nathaniel's tone carried a warning.

But Jules ignored him completely, cackling as he pushed into the center of the crowd.

"Nathaniel. I genuinely love the tacky little economy you've built here. The debt traps, the monopoly, printing vouchers! Classic Wall Street garbage—it's perfect. But your system has one fatal flaw."

Jules flicked open the briefcase latch.

"You assumed you could monopolize the right to issue currency. Forgetting that this world is completely and utterly finished."

Jules flipped the briefcase upside down and shook it violently toward the sky.

FSSSHHHHH—

In an instant, hundreds—thousands—of paper slips cascaded into the ashen air like a blizzard. Every one of them was a Water Voucher, identical to those Nathaniel had been issuing as his self-appointed central bank.

The red "N.P." initials stamped at the center. The paper stock, the dimensions, even Nathaniel's distinctive handwriting—perfect replicas. They spiraled on the wind and scattered across the mud-and-blood-soaked ground.

"What… what is all that?"

The billionaires' eyes went wide. The absolute currency—the thing they had bled to protect, the thing that could only be obtained through fourteen hours of bone-grinding labor—was tumbling through the filth like garbage.

For the first time, Nathaniel's elegant composure shattered into something bestial.

"You insane bastard…! What have you done!"

Jules doubled over, clutching his sides, and howled with laughter.

"I told you I was dismantling the broken drone's components, didn't I? Inside it was a precision laser engraving module! I cut up the premium vellum I brought from the bunker and printed thousands of copies of your pathetic signature and stamp. How's that for Silicon Valley craftsmanship?"

The camp detonated into chaos.

Counterfeit currency. Or rather, in a lawless wasteland with no central regulatory authority, it was not counterfeiting at all—it was a flawless act of quantitative easing. Helicopter money, scattered from the sky.

"Money! It's vouchers!"
"Three of these and I can pay off all my debt!"

The very billionaires who moments ago had been trembling before a bloodied club now threw themselves flat in the mud, clawing at the soiled vouchers in a frenzy. Even Richard—drenched in his own blood—was stuffing paper slips into his mouth with his one functional hand. Preston tried to swing his club, but dozens of wild-eyed men were beyond stopping. He was trampled underfoot in seconds.

"STOP! Everyone stop! Those are fakes! They're counterfeit!"

Arthur hurled his bark ledger and screamed, but the mania of a market that had lost all reason could not be halted.

David stood in the rain of falling currency and felt a chill of catharsis.

No matter how brutal the dictatorship, no matter how perfect the legal framework—the most terrifying natural enemy of monopoly capitalism is always uncontrollable inflation. The moment money floods the market like garbage, the currency's value converges to zero, and every debtor's chains turn to confetti.

The pyramid of power Nathaniel had meticulously constructed with dozens of loan affidavits was shattering to pieces—demolished by a single act of madness from the joker.

"So, Nathaniel!"

Jules flung fistfuls of paper into the air and crowed.

"Now that the market is drowning in vouchers, how exactly do you plan to meet the demand with that dinky little purifier of yours? Congratulations on your bank run!"

In the frenzied forest, a single blood-smeared counterfeit voucher fluttered down onto Nathaniel's mud-streaked face. The perfectly controlled society of Act One was collapsing, and the ungovernable chaos of Act Two was raising its spectacular curtain.
