The Explosion

The blast was as though a fragment of the sun's surface had been torn loose and rammed into the center of the compound.

The frenzied surplus power from the billionaires—funneled through the single point of failure—thrashed inside the main switchboard whose control valves Chloe had severed, found no outlet, and finally breached its physical limits.

BOOOOOOOM—!

With an eardrum-splitting roar, the shipping container housing the main switchboard was engulfed in a colossal fireball and white flash, and launched skyward. Thick copper cables snapped like whips and lashed through the air; shrapnel of shattered steel rained down in every direction like shotgun pellets.

The perfect military might of the neo-humans who had controlled the compound shattered along with the power grid they had placed their faith in. The plasma-cutter spears in their hands could not withstand the surge of ultra-high-voltage current feeding back through the centralized grid and detonated in a chain reaction.

"GRAAAAAH!"

Bestial screams shook the compound. The neo-humans at the vanguard, those swinging clubs, were caught in the blast wave of their own exploding weapons—limbs torn away. Even the Alpha, with his overwhelming frame, was hurled by the container's detonation into a heap of building debris. The iron fortress, which had seemed impregnable, was reduced to perfect chaos by a single circuit overload.

And around the capstans—closest to the epicenter of the explosion.
The billionaires who had been driving the gears in a frenzy were bowled over by the shockwave and slammed into the mud. The massive hand-cranked gear's teeth shattered, and the yokes and chains binding them snapped free, one by one.

"Hff… hff…"

Preston Huntington III lay there, blood trickling from his ears, too stunned to wipe it away, staring up at the night sky with vacant eyes. The flames of the burning compound flickered red in his bloodshot pupils.

The chains were broken. The monsters' weapons were destroyed, and they lay bleeding on the ground. David Sterling had been right. The extreme leverage they had offered up had collapsed the system, and they had won their freedom.

"We… we did it. We won!"

Cohen, the former resort magnate, was crawling across the floor, screaming in deranged triumph. Arthur Lindley, who had collapsed coughing blood, and the other billionaires were letting out grotesque, broken laughter. All they had to do now was rise on their shredded feet and walk out through the gates of this demolished slaughterhouse.

Preston planted his hands and tried to stand.
But his body would not move.

"Wh—?"

Preston's legs had locked rigid as logs, refusing even to twitch. When he forced himself upright, a grotesque wet rupture sounded from his knee joint, and an excruciating pain detonated through his entire body.

"AAAAAAGH!"
Preston crashed back into the mud. Cohen was no different. He tried to raise his arms in celebration, but before he could lift them he clutched his chest, foaming at the mouth, convulsing. Every one of the jubilant billionaires collapsed where they stood, panting like beasts.

Only then did Preston understand what had happened to his body.
Blinded by the cheap incentive of a bonus and freedom, they had sprinted far past the breaking point of their flesh. Their muscle fibers had not merely torn—they had liquefied. Cartilage was pulverized. Cardiac muscle was on the verge of bursting from overload.

They had not won their freedom. They had been perfectly consumed—single-use biological detonators, expended to blow up the neo-humans' compound and then discarded.

"Sterling… David Sterling! You goddamn fraud!"

Preston, bloody tears streaming down his face, howled into the darkness beyond the raging flames. But David was nowhere to be seen—not coming to rescue them, not coming to share the dividends of this so-called victory.

The most horrifying truth of capitalism: when the great bubble bursts and the market collapses, the architects who designed the party are always the first ones through the emergency exit. Only the retail investors—the ones who, intoxicated by the fantasy, scraped together every last scrap of their souls to take on debt—are left standing in the burning market, absorbing the full weight of every loss with their own bodies. David had taken the exact rules of deceitful exploitation the billionaires had spent a lifetime using against the lower classes, and reflected them back with surgical precision.

"Ha ha ha ha! THIS is it! This is what I'm talking about!"

From the burning wreckage of the container, a lunatic burst of laughter erupted. Julian Cross, caught in the explosion, was covered in burns and streaming blood, and yet he was rolling on the ground, screaming with euphoria.

"Perfect entropy! A perfect catastrophe! Behold the beautiful spectacle of order collapsing!"

Against the backdrop of Jules's deranged laughter, grotesque metallic sounds began to stir within the flames—the neo-humans were rising again.

Their weapons were destroyed and they were wounded, but the neo-humans were fundamentally built of different material than the hothouse flowers of the billionaire class. The Alpha ripped the blood-soaked bone mask from his face and surged atop the building debris. On his hideously scarred bare face, the rational calculation of a resource-efficient predator had been replaced by the boiling fury of a killer stripped of all reason.

The Alpha raised a massive saw-toothed club and roared. At that signal, a dozen surviving neo-humans, gripping crowbars and butcher's hooks, began advancing on the billionaires lying immobile on the ground.

"No… don't come… please, someone help!"
Preston tried to drag himself away on a shattered leg, but there was nowhere to go on the mud floor. The Alpha's colossal shadow fell over him. The final destination for the speculators who had been promised dividends was a merciless abattoir—alive, flesh stripped from bone beneath the saw-toothed blades of predators who had lost all reason.

At that same moment, beyond the compound's rear exhaust duct—

David and Chloe were slipping through the black smoke billowing from the compound and vanishing into the deep darkness of the forest. Behind them, the thunderous collapse of the compound and the billionaires' dying screams tore into the night sky, but neither of them looked back. Not once.

"In the end, they signed their own ledger of greed with their own blood."

Chloe gasped for breath as she pushed through the forest.

David snapped a blood-smeared branch from his path and walked on in silence. Inside the vast Excel sheet that occupied his mind, the variable labeled "100 billionaires" had been perfectly liquidated, converging to zero. They had not been the protagonists driving the system—they had fulfilled their role as expendable data points, used to prove the system's fatal error.

But the forest was not finished.
The flames of the burning compound behind them paled in comparison to what lay ahead: the forest stretching before the two of them was grotesquely silent and cold. As though the carnage that had just occurred were nothing more than a game played inside a miniature box, the vast, primordial abyss of nature was opening its maw to receive them.

"The true wild is a place where neither the rules of capitalism nor the logic of overload hold any power."
David whispered, dissolving into the darkness.

The arrogant empire erected by false gods had burned. The deformed predators who had evolved in this forest were shackled within those flames. Now, with no ledger, no document, no cheap deception left to rely on, two people's long, savage final struggle for survival was beginning—in the deepest essence of an annihilated Earth.
