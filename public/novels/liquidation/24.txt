24
Death Leverage and the Bloody Performance Bonus

GRRRRIND—CLANG!

The sound of massive rusted gears meshing and turning was indistinguishable from the screaming of hell. At the center of the neo-human forward operating base, blood from the billionaires' torn soles had already pooled in circular arcs beneath the hand-cranked capstans they were pushing.

Worse than the crushing weight of the wooden yokes on their shoulders was the lung-shredding oxygen deprivation and the wet ripping of muscle that surged with every revolution. Cohen was already half-unconscious, foam bubbling from his mouth, but the chain around his neck would not let him fall—he was dragged along the ground, forced into his orbit.

Chained at the very front of that infernal tableau was Preston Huntington III.
The meager stamina of the "number-one gladiator"—who, days earlier, had swung a cudgel and plundered at will—had bottomed out in mere hours under the neo-humans' merciless lashing. Preston's pupils were unfocused; nothing escaped his lips but the panting of a beast. *If only my heart would burst and end this.* That was Preston's sole remaining wish.

Then—as the capstan rotated through a dark blind spot of the compound—

"Preston."

From beneath the grate of a ventilation duct, deep in shadow, came a low, arid voice. Preston stared into the darkness with vacant eyes. There, crouched inside a pipe joint with mud smeared across his face, David Sterling was looking down at him.

"St—Sterling…? Help me… please, get me out of here…"
Preston whispered, trembling under his yoke.

"I have no intention of getting you out. You people mortgaged your own cartilage. That was your decision."
David's voice held not a shred of sympathy.

"What I have brought you is a piece of insider information with an exceptionally high rate of return."

*Insider information.*
In this abattoir, hearing that antiquated term from the world of finance caused a bizarre twitch in Preston's dying neural pathways.

"You're aware that Jules integrated the compound's switchboard into a centralized grid? Thanks to that, the monsters' plasma spears are now charging at maximum output. But Jules left one critical flaw in place."

David steadied his breathing and drove the perfect lie—or rather, the perfect half-truth—into the greedy recesses of Preston's brain.

"This turbine regulates voltage stably when you walk at a constant speed. But what happens if you push the capstan at twice—no, three times your current speed, hard enough to trigger an instantaneous overload? The main switchboard blows, and every weapon and electronic control system connected to it detonates with it."

David's eyes glittered like a serpent's.
"That means the wrists of the monsters torturing you get blown off, and the compound's gates fly open. This is the perfect leveraged investment: you grind your bones and flesh to one hundred percent, and you receive freedom and revenge at a one-thousand-percent dividend. Pure leverage."

A bolt of shock lanced through Preston's dull eyes.
On Wall Street, he had been a speculator who hunted corporations by borrowing ten billion dollars against one billion in principal. *Squeeze out everything you've got and rake in the pot on a single hand.* The cheap performance-bonus logic David had lobbed landed with surgical precision on the gambling instinct etched into Preston's marrow.

"Over… overload… explosion…"
A grotesque grin seeped between Preston's cracked lips.

"Spread the word to the others. Now. Increase the pace gradually so they don't notice, then on my signal, sprint at full power. Tell them it's the only chance to burn these monsters alive."

The capstan rotated back into the light, and David's form dissolved into darkness.

Preston clenched the yoke so tightly that blood returned to his whitened knuckles. He growled like an animal at Arthur Lindley and Cohen staggering behind him.

"Run… if we run, we can kill them all… trigger the overload and we win…!"

That clandestine, lethal piece of intelligence spread like a contagion along the chain, whispering from ear to ear.
*Run and you survive. Burn the monsters alive. This is our final short squeeze—the moment the shorts get crushed and we reclaim everything!*

In the age of civilization, these men had deceived their workers: "Hit a hundred and twenty percent of quota and you'll receive a special bonus." When workers coughed blood to meet the target, these same men had quietly rewritten the benchmarks and pocketed the bonus themselves.
And now, the thin, deceitful "special bonus" David had dangled was fooling the very billionaires who had considered themselves the smartest people on Earth. Why? Because at the absolute bottom of despair, they had been reduced, perfectly and completely, to workers clutching at any straw of hope.

"AAAAAAAAAGH!"

Preston unleashed an insane war cry and hurled himself against the yoke.
"PUSH! PUSH IT NOW, YOU SONS OF BITCHES!"

What happened next was a miracle no one would have believed possible from men who had been dying moments ago. Cohen, foam still spilling from his mouth, locked his legs and drove. Arthur Lindley clenched his jaw until his teeth cracked and shoved against the steel column. The billionaires, eyes rolled back like zealots in a frenzy, began sprinting as they pushed the capstan.

GRRRRRRRRIND—CLANG! CLANG!

The gears, which had been turning with sluggish weight, screamed as they accelerated to a manic velocity. The turbine's RPM spiked violently, and a grotesque vibration began radiating through the entire compound.

The one most shocked by the sudden frenzy was Nathaniel Pierce, still chained within the line.
Nathaniel had not heard David's message. Yanked by the neck-chain of the billionaires who had suddenly begun sprinting in front of him, he was tumbling and being dragged across the dirt.

"Wh—what are you doing! Stop! Your hearts will burst! Stop, you idiots!"
Nathaniel screamed as his face was ground against the floor, but the billionaires—drunk on the fantasy of leverage—heard nothing. They ran even as their muscles tore, even as blood poured from their mouths. This was not the survival instinct of an animal. It was the madness of speculators addicted to returns.

In one corner of the compound, inside the container housing the switchboard, Julian Cross—who had been celebrating—cocked his head in confusion.
"Huh? Why's the voltage doing that? Did those people just pop some kind of stimulant?"

The power surging from the turbines was flooding into the main switchboard Jules had just consolidated at a lunatic rate. The switchboard's gauge needle rocketed toward the red zone.

The neo-human guards, alarmed, charged in swinging their clubs, but the billionaires did not stop—not even when electric shocks ripped through them. The fantasy of vengeance was larger than the pain.

"Jules! Shut it down! It's overloading!"
The neo-human Alpha, sensing the anomaly, let out a bestial roar and lunged toward the switchboard. Jules, panicking, grabbed for the main circuit breaker.

But the breaker would not budge.
Outside the container, along the cooling pipeline—Chloe Bennett was gasping for breath, having severed the switchboard's safety-control valve and the breaker's relay wire with a pair of industrial nippers.

"The safety systems are dead, you monsters."
Chloe stuffed the severed wires into the mud and whispered, cold as ice.

WHIIIIIIIIIIIRR—SCREEEEEE!

The main switchboard inhaled a surge of power it was never designed to hold and emitted a grotesque, building roar. Jules scrambled backward. The blue sparks on the neo-humans' plasma spears lost all stability and began arcing wildly in every direction like fireworks gone haywire.

"It's blowing…! It's going to blow!"
Preston screamed in blood-soaked euphoria. They had succeeded—burning their own cartilage and hearts as fuel to deliver an irreversible bomb to the compound's heart.

David Sterling rose quietly from behind the exhaust-duct grate, watching the perfect catastrophe unfold.
The "infinite growth" and "excess returns" the capitalists had worshiped their entire lives ended, as they always must, in a system-wide explosion triggered by an uncontrollable overload. The last drops of blood and sweat from the billionaires concentrated on a single point of failure were being transmuted into a blinding light that would devour the neo-humans' peak-efficiency empire.

"Time for liquidation."
With David's dry pronouncement, a white flash brighter than the sun erupted from the main switchboard.
