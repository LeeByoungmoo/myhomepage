29
The Algorithm's Merciless Sorting

What poured through the gaping hole in the earth was more than grey fog.

KRRRUMBLE—CRASH!

Through the ceiling hatch, dozens of meters above, massive forms plummeted alongside bestial screams, slamming mercilessly onto the warehouse's concrete floor. The three-meter obsidian-plated creature. Neo-human warriors, weapons still in hand, thrashing in midair.

On the surface, they had been the undisputed apex of a flawless food chain. But gravity extended them no courtesy. The creature that cratered into the floor let out a grotesque shriek as bones shattered on impact. Several neo-humans, unable to survive the fall, were reduced to blood-soaked slabs of meat on arrival.

But the place they had fallen into was not the bottom of any ecosystem. It was the dead center of the coldest, most perfect digestive organ of capitalism that human greed had ever created: the hyper-automated logistics center.

WHIIIIIRR—BEEP! BEEP!

Before the creature's roar had faded, a mechanical cacophony erupted across the tens of thousands of square meters of warehouse space. Awakened by the bankruptcy-liquidation protocol David had entered, thousands of unmanned forklifts and the insectoid steel robotic arms dangling from the ceiling swiveled in unison toward the massive "organic waste" that had dropped from above.

Red laser scanners at the tips of the robotic arms raked cruciform patterns across the bodies of the creature and the neo-humans.

[Barcode unrecognized. Classified as Bad Inventory.]
[Asset Value: 0. Executing physical shredding and incineration procedures.]

The instant cold green text surfaced on the control-room screen, the massacre began.

The first target was the colossal radiation-mutated creature. Dozens of heavy-duty robotic arms, having identified its bulk as "oversized defective freight," converged from every direction like serpents.

KAAAAAAH!

The creature flashed its yellow thermal-sensor eyes and opened its maw. A jet of highly toxic acid from its esophagus scored a direct hit on three incoming robotic arms. Steel frames hissed and melted black; internal circuits detonated in showers of sparks.

On the surface, any living thing would have recoiled from that overwhelming attack. But these machines possessed no survival instinct, no fear—no "emotional inefficiency" of any kind. The instant the three destroyed arms hit the floor, six new arms waiting in reserve replaced them with zero-point-one seconds of latency, driving titanium pincers into the creature's torso.

CRUNCH—SHRIEEEK!

"Dear God…"
Chloe Bennett, watching through the control room's reinforced glass, could not contain her shock.

The titanium pincers punched through the creature's obsidian exoskeleton and wrenched its joints apart without mercy. The creature thrashed and swung its tail, smashing robot bodies—but the machines kept coming in waves. Flesh was torn away; bodily fluid sprayed in every direction. The limitations inherent to a living organism—pain and the ceiling of stamina—were utterly meaningless against the algorithm's attrition strategy, which regenerated endlessly by swapping out components.

Meanwhile, the neo-human Alpha and his surviving warriors, barely alive after the fall, raised their plasma-cutter spears inside this deranged hellscape.

"Destroy them all!"
The Alpha roared in a metallic, bestial voice and swung his plasma spear at an oncoming unmanned forklift. The ultra-high-temperature blue flash cleaved the forklift's front section clean in two.

But that was as far as the Alpha's counterattack went.

The warehouse's master algorithm, detecting battery-fluid leakage and signs of fire from the destroyed forklift, reclassified the neo-humans as "flammable hazardous material." From dark crevices in the ceiling, colossal hydraulic presses—originally designed to compress shipping boxes—descended spider-like, thudding against the floor.

As the Alpha raised his spear for another swing, steel cargo-securing bands launched from every direction, coiling around his limbs and hoisting him into the air.

"GRAAAAAH! Release me!"
The Alpha strained with superhuman force to snap the bands, but the tensile strength of industrial-grade steel strapping was not something muscle could overcome. Suspended spread-eagle, the Alpha looked up—and the shadow of a hydraulic press plate weighing dozens of tons crept over his skull, descending with slow, mechanical inevitability.

WHIIIIRR—CRUNCH!

There was not even a scream. The Alpha's two-and-a-half-meter frame was crushed against the floor—flattened like packing material compressed to eliminate dead space in a shipping box. The hideous wet crack of bursting bone and flesh was perfectly drowned out by the ponderous din of the machinery.

The remaining neo-human warriors met the same fate. Their bodies were tossed onto the conveyor belts of unmanned sorters and carried at precise, unerring speed toward industrial shredders designed to grind animal bone to powder.

"This is the ultimate ecosystem capitalism ever produced."

David Sterling stood beneath the control room's cold blue light, his forehead pressed against the reinforced glass, watching the overwhelming carnage with arid detachment.

"Wild predators stop hunting when their bellies are full. The apex hunters of the forest know when to retreat before superior force. But algorithms are different. They feel neither hunger nor fear. They simply repeat the prescribed protocol on an infinite loop—until the inventory count on the spreadsheet reaches zero."

In David's eyes, the conveyor belts ran red, stained with blood and bodily fluid.

These cold steel gears—invented to replace human labor and slash costs—were now processing even the most powerful life-forms humanity's evolution had produced. This was a dimension apart from Nathaniel's fraudulent ledger or the billionaires' cheap speculation. The mechanical cathedral built by capitalists who abhorred the anguish of physical labor had, in the cruelest of ironies, become the most blood-soaked slaughterhouse on Earth—sorting organic matter for disposal.

BEEP—BEEEEEEP!

Then the control room's central screen flashed red once more and raised a new warning.

[Surplus Inventory Shredding Rate: 87% reached.]
[Contamination level exceeds hazard threshold. Biological fluid leakage detected within facility.]
[Initiating final stage of Liquidation Protocol: Sector C full high-temperature incineration and sterilization procedures.]
[Time remaining: 10:00.]

Chloe's face drained of all color.
"Incineration?! The system has classified the monsters' blood and acid as facility contaminants. In ten minutes, it's going to seal every ventilation duct in this warehouse and blast the interior with eight-hundred-degree flames to achieve complete sterilization!"

Chloe hammered at the keyboard in a frenzy, but the screen was immovable.
"No… the final stage of the liquidation protocol can't be cancelled manually. The control room is inside the incineration perimeter. We're going to be roasted alive in here!"

Capital's ledger-clearing makes no exceptions.
When incinerating surplus assets that have caused a system error, Chapter 11's ultimate purpose is to burn everything—the robotic arms that shredded the assets and every trace of contamination—reducing the balance sheet to a perfect zero. The reaper David had summoned was now aiming its scythe at their own necks.

"Is there an exit? An emergency evacuation route for executives or VIPs who visited for inspections—something like that."
David asked with perfect composure.

Chloe frantically swiped through the warehouse schematics on her tablet.
"Yes! At the very back of the facility, behind the main server room—there's a direct executive elevator. It runs on its own independent power supply, so it won't be affected by the incineration protocol. It goes straight up to the bunker's surface safe zone in a single ride."

"Then that's where we go."

"The problem is the location!"
Chloe projected the schematic onto the reinforced glass and cried out in despair.

"To reach that elevator, we have to leave the control room, cross the main conveyor-belt sorting floor head-on—the one where those robots are tearing apart every organic thing in sight!"

David's gaze shifted from the schematic to the reality beyond the control room.
On the other side of the reinforced glass, the half-bisected creature was still spewing acid and battling the robots. Severed neo-human limbs rode conveyor belts toward the smelter. And above them all, thousands of red laser scanners buzzed like swarms of flies, hunting for the next target to shred.

To survive, they would have to cross that killing field on foot, unprotected.

"There's no time."
David picked up a pipe wrench that had fallen to the floor and spoke dryly.

"In the capitalist market, the most perfect exit—the recovery of your investment—always happens in the final ten minutes before the market collapses to ash. We need to find a way to fool the algorithm's eyes and cross that slaughterhouse."

On the ceiling, the red countdown timer ticked downward.
Heat was already beginning to radiate from the control-room floor. Inside the coldest, most merciless incinerator of a ledger ever built, the two of them were about to begin their final, life-or-death runaway.
