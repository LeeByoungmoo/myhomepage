# PRESSURE
## Episode 11: Other Houses, Other Hells

---

Tragedy pretends to be fair to everyone, but it wears a different face in every household. 

While the Han family was entering a new phase following Ian's awakening and Emma's confession, other families across the city were also suffering from the invisible poison of Pressure, each sick in their own unique way. 

***

[The Choi Family - The Perfect House]

"Wake up."

5:00 AM. Instead of an alarm clock, a low, heavy voice echoed through the hallway of the two-story suburban home. 
The father exhaled, and his overwhelming Level 9 Pressure filled every corner of the house. 

One minute later, two teenage boys stood at attention in the living room, wearing sharply pressed school uniforms. The older boy was a Level 7, the younger a Level 6. They were considered elites even at the Top-Tier academy, but in front of their father, they stood like terrified recruits in boot camp. 

"Control your Pressure perfectly today, as always. If you tarnish our family's name, I will cut you out without hesitation."

"Yes, sir. Understood." 
The two sons answered in perfect unison. 

The father nodded and walked toward the kitchen. The mother, a Level 2, was preparing breakfast. At the sound of her husband's footsteps, her shoulders instinctively stiffened. 

"The soup is salty."
The father took a spoonful of the broth and stated drily. 

The mother didn't make an excuse. In fact, for the last ten years, she hadn't spoken a single word freely in front of her husband. The difference in Pressure was the difference in power, and a conversation simply could not exist between a Level 9 and a Level 2. She quietly took the soup bowl away and replaced it with a glass of tap water. 

From the outside, the Choi family was the most outstanding, model family of Pressure users. But on the inside, it was just a massive military barracks, suppressed by the sheer violence of Pressure. 

***

[The Yoon Family - The Inverted House]

"No! I'm not going to kindergarten! I wanna watch cartoons!"

A deafening scream erupted in the living room of a luxury penthouse. 
Tyler Yoon, five years old. Three months ago, while going down a slide at the kindergarten playground, this child had awakened as a Level 7. 

*BANG!*

As Tyler threw himself on the floor and threw a tantrum, the expensive marble flooring of the penthouse began to crack and splinter. The living room sofas hovered in the air, and a painting worth tens of thousands of dollars fell off the wall and shattered. 

"Tyler, sweetie, please... Mommy will put the cartoons on, okay? So please, stop being mad..."

Tyler's mother (Level 3) and father (Level 4) were on their knees in front of the child, begging. The mother hurriedly pressed the TV remote with shaking hands, while the father frantically swept up the broken glass with his bare hands, terrified the child might get a scratch. 

The five-year-old child was the king of the house. 
The parents couldn't discipline him. If they tried to discipline him, he got angry. If he got angry, the building collapsed. They weren't 'raising' a child; they were 'serving' a master. Begging for their lives every single day. 

***

[The Park Family - The Normal House]

"Ugh, seriously! You used all my body wash again!"
"I didn't use it! And anyway, that's the shared one!"
"Are you crazy? It's cherry scented! That's my personal bottle!"

7:00 AM. Loud shouting echoed from the second floor of a modest townhouse. 
Leo Park and his younger sister were having a vicious shouting match through the closed bathroom door. 

"Hey! Both of you shut up! You're embarrassing us in front of the whole neighborhood first thing in the morning!"
Their mom, who was flipping pancakes in the kitchen, walked out holding a spatula and yelled. 

"Mom, tell him to stop! He's always sneaking my stuff!"
"I told you it wasn't me! Oh my god, this is so unfair."

Their dad was sitting in the living room in his undershirt, reading the paper and just chuckling at the chaos. 

Everyone in Leo's family had similar Pressure levels, hovering between Level 3 and 4. Nobody was exceptionally strong, and nobody was exceptionally weak. 

Because of that, they could fight. 
Instead of crushing each other with Pressure, they raised their voices, argued, got annoyed, and sometimes made up and found a compromise. 

They were loud, messy, and imperfect. 
But they were also the only house in the city that maintained the shape of a 'real family.' 

***

That afternoon, Ian had been invited to Leo's house for dinner. 

"Man, you're all over the news lately. My mom was going crazy telling me to get your autograph when you came over."
Leo said, chewing on a piece of fried chicken. 

"An autograph? Yeah, right."
Ian chuckled and grabbed a slice of pizza. Since that night in Emma's room when he had hugged her, Ian's expression had softened significantly. 

Watching Leo's family eat dinner, Ian felt a warm ache in his chest. 
Stealing food off each other's plates, getting nagged by their mom, their dad making lame dad jokes—it was such a perfectly normal scene. 

*Could our family... ever be like that?*

Walking home after dinner. 
As Ian turned the corner of a dark alley, he bumped into someone. 

A woman in a dark trench coat was standing under the streetlight. 
The air around her was flowing very strangely. It was a strong Pressure, but it was so thoroughly concealed that he couldn't read its texture. 

"Ian."
The woman spoke in a smooth, quiet voice. 

"Who are you?"
Ian asked, going on alert. 

"I'm the leader of the Liberators. My name is Evelyn Thorne. You didn't reply to my text message, so I came to find you myself."

Ian's eyes turned cold. 
"I have no interest in the government, and I have no interest in you people. Go away."

Evelyn Thorne didn't back down. She slowly stepped toward Ian and said, 

"You will join us eventually. Because you already know."

She whispered right next to his ear. 

"You know exactly how the prison called 'Family' is making Pressure users sick. And until we tear that down, there is no true salvation in this world."

Ian's eyes wavered slightly. 
Her words were picking at the deepest, most painful wound in his heart.
