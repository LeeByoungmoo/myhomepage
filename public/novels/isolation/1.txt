# PRESSURE
## Episode 1: The Weight of Air

---

Mornings in the family home began by weighing the air. 

It wasn’t a metaphor. The moment sixteen-year-old Ian opened his eyes, he inhaled, testing the barometric pressure in his room. If the air filling his lungs felt heavy and thick, it meant his sister, Emma—sleeping just one wall away—was in a bad mood. If the air was light and cool, they might survive a normal day. And if the air pricked his skin like needles... someone was going to bleed.

Today, the air was trembling. 

Ian kicked off his blanket without making a sound. He walked on his tiptoes and opened the door. The hallway was dead silent, but an invisible weight was already crushing the suburban house. 

He stepped into the kitchen. His mother, Sarah, was standing by the stove making breakfast. Ian didn't miss the faint tremor in her fingertips. Her Pressure was Level 4. For an average adult woman, it was enough to live a normal, functioning life. In this house, however, it was a meaningless number. 

"Mom." 

Ian whispered. Sarah flinched and whipped around. 

"Ian, you’re up? Watch your step. You'll wake Emma." 
"She’s already awake. The air is heavy."

Deep exhaustion flashed across Sarah’s face. Without a word, she went back to flipping pancakes. At that moment, the master bedroom door opened, and his father, David, shuffled out. A middle-aged man with perpetually slumped shoulders. Once, he was known as a Level 6 'Enforcer' working for the federal government. Now, he was a lethargic patriarch hiding behind a pathetic Level 2, constantly walking on eggshells around his own family. 

"Good morning," David forced a bright tone.

Then it happened. 

*Vwoooom—* 

A low vibration rattled the entire house. Emma’s bedroom door swung open. Twelve years old. A young girl with a childish face stepped out into the living room. With every step she took, the dust in the air plummeted to the hardwood floor. 

"You're loud." 

Emma snapped, annoyance dripping from her voice. 

With those two words, the air in the kitchen seemingly compressed. Level 8. An ultra-high-risk Pressure user—a classification given to less than 0.1% of the population. This uncontrollable monster was the reigning tyrant of their home. 

David coughed awkwardly and glued his eyes to the floor. Sarah hurriedly pushed a plate of pancakes—Emma’s favorite—in front of her daughter's seat at the kitchen island. 

"Emma, sweetie, did you sleep well? Come eat." 

Emma pulled out a barstool and sat. The screech of the metal legs against the tile echoed unnaturally loud. Four-year-old Sam, the youngest, held his breath at the far end of the island, nervously picking at a piece of bacon. 

Breakfast was a negotiation on thin ice. If Emma complained about the syrup being too cold, the windows threatened to crack. If Emma smiled, everyone had to force a smile in relief. 

Ian just shoved food into his mouth, silently working his fork. He was the only 'Null' in the family—a Level 1. In a household ruled by raw power, Ian was basically invisible. Whether she despised him or simply didn't care enough to notice him, Emma didn't spare her older brother a single glance.

"I'm leaving."

Fleeing the house, Ian finally let out the long breath he had been holding the second the front door clicked shut. 

The world outside wasn't much different. Pedestrians unconsciously sensed each other's Pressure and adjusted their distance. Your Level wasn't printed on a driver's license, but everyone knew instinctively. The strong and the weak could be distinguished just by the flow of the air.

School was a microcosm of this brutal ecosystem. 

Ian attended a 'Low-Tier' public high school, an underfunded dumping ground exclusively for kids between Levels 1 and 3. The exterior was dreary brick, the lockers dented and rusting. It stood in stark, pathetic contrast to the gleaming glass-and-steel architecture of the 'Top-Tier' academy (Level 7 and above) towering across the street.

"Hey, Null. Survived the morning?"

Someone clapped his shoulder as he passed the chain-link gates. It was Leo, his only friend. Leo was also a Level 1 Null, but unlike Ian, he armed himself with cynicism and bitter humor against the world. 

"Yeah. Still breathing."
"Your sister throw another tantrum?"
"Almost split the kitchen counter in half because her pancakes weren't perfectly round."

Leo clicked his tongue. "World's gone insane. A twelve-year-old brat sits at the head of the table and makes her whole family shake in their boots? Why the hell does the government leave monsters like that in the suburbs?"

"They want to keep up the illusion of the 'nuclear family'." 

Ian replied dryly. 

Classes were a blur of boredom. The teachers didn't expect much from Low-Tier kids anyway. The best these kids could hope for after graduation was doing manual labor in the shadows of high-level users, or shuffling papers in a cubicle. 

Ian stared out the window. Across the street, the massive dome of the Top-Tier academy's training arena was sliding open. 

*If only I had power.*

Every now and then, Ian indulged in that useless fantasy. If he had an overwhelming Pressure that could crush Emma, could he break the chains of his hellish home? Would his father lift his head again? Would his mother finally be able to smile without fear?

But the fantasy always ended in a chilling dread. 

*No. If I had power, I'd probably become a monster too.*

The walk home from school always felt like a death march. Today, his finger hesitated a fraction of a second longer over the keypad on the front door. 

*Beep-beep-beep.* 

The door unlocked. Ian stepped in, immediately weighing the air out of pure habit. 

It was cool. So quiet it felt ominous. 

"I'm home."

Silence. Ian cautiously stepped into the living room. Emma wasn't there; Sam had fallen asleep on the rug, clutching an action figure. 

And at the kitchen island.
His mother, Sarah, sat staring blankly into space. 

"Mom?"

Ian approached to touch her shoulder. Sarah flinched violently, shrinking away from him. As she turned her head, Ian's heart dropped to his stomach. 

Her right cheek was swollen and red. Subcutaneous bleeding was already forming a dark bruise. It wasn't a normal bruise from a physical strike. It was the telltale sign of ruptured capillaries caused by a localized shockwave of high Pressure. 

"Mom... this."

Sarah quickly bowed her head, using her blonde hair to hide the injury. 

"It's nothing. Just... an accident. Emma lost control for a second..." 

Her voice trembled. 

Ian clenched his fists. His fingernails dug into his palms until it hurt. Rage boiled up from the pit of his stomach, but there was absolutely nothing he could do. 

He was a Level 1. 
Being powerless meant he didn't even have the right to get angry when the person he loved was hurt. 

That night, for the first time in his life, Ian prayed. 
He begged for teeth. Just a sliver of power, anything to fight back against the monster in the next room. 

No one knew yet that his prayer would be answered in the most horrifying way possible.
