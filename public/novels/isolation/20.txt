## Episode 20: Cradle of the Predator (Final Episode)

Crunch.

The sound of our only hope of survival shattering under a rough dress shoe pierced my eardrums.

Through the acrid smoke rising from the iron grate, the hem of a perfectly ironed, top-tier tailored suit was revealed under the glaring lights.

"You've worked hard, Madam CEO. I had to empty my entire life's retirement fund to drag you down to the bottom of this hell."

The moment I saw the face of the man stepping out of the shadows, I forgot the agonizing pain in my torn thigh and burst into a bizarre, hollow laugh.

It was my Chief Secretary—my most loyal shadow, the man who had silently executed every single one of my dirty orders for the past twenty years.

My husband's revenge, my rival's grudge, my son's hatred.

The real spider manipulating all of them from the shadows, dragging us into this massive slaughterhouse, had been standing right next to me the entire time.

"Why are you laughing? This is the perfect grave built by the bugs you crushed, just so they could tear you apart."

The Chief Secretary aimed a cold gun barrel at the center of my forehead and asked dryly.

"Perfect?"

I slowly lifted my blood-soaked face and flashed him a chillingly bright smile.

Instead of the cliché of a weakling begging for mercy in front of a gun, I squeezed the handle of my hunting knife, my grip slippery with blood and sweat.

"No matter how many bugs gather, they're still just beneath a predator's feet."

Before the sentence even finished, I kicked off the broken grate and sprang up like a beast.

The panicked Secretary pulled the trigger, but the bullet only grazed the edge of my thin cardigan and vanished into the air. I slipped into that 0.1-second margin of error and ruthlessly drove my cold blade into the back of his knee.

"Gaaaargh!"

With a torn joint, the Secretary screamed and collapsed to the floor.

I kicked the gun out of his hand, and without hesitation, buried the knife deep into his neck.

Hot blood erupted like a geyser, dying my face completely red.

Panting heavily, I limped past his body toward the dark passage he had emerged from. There, a small, old service elevator leading to the surface was waiting with its doors open.

Clank, clatter.

The elevator began its ascent with a heavy, mechanical groan.

Beneath my feet, my husband's horrific screams, the howling of the fighting dogs, and the smell of my son's burning flesh were tangled together like hell as they awaited permanent burial.

Ding.

A cheerful chime rang out, and the elevator doors opened.

The piercing sunlight, the loud honking of cars, and the indifferent footsteps of a bustling crowd. It was the perfectly peaceful center of downtown Seoul.

I wiped the blood off my face with the sleeve of my thin cardigan.

Hiding my limp, I put on my usual elegant, perfectly controlled smile and walked right into the blinding crowd. Monsters never stay in hell. They just slip back into the world of humans to pick their next leash.
