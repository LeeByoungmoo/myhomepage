# PRESSURE
## Episode 15: Conversations Without Armor

---

The air in the living room was heavy, but it was no longer because of violent 'Pressure.' 
It was because of the weight of the truth—the weight of having to drag out nine years of accumulated lies and scars. 

Ian was the one to break the silence. 
His gaze moved to his father, David, sitting across from him. 

"Dad."
Ian's voice was perfectly calm. 

"You used to be a Level 6 Enforcer. When Emma first started losing control, you were more than strong enough to restrain her. But instead, you injected yourself with a serum that permanently lowered your Pressure. Why did you do that?"

David's shoulders shook noticeably. He tried to look away, but Ian didn't back down. 

"Because you made yourself weak, our house fell under Emma's control and turned into a living hell. Why didn't you protect us?"

David rubbed his face with his hands. A deep, agonizing grief was etched into his wrinkled face. 

"If I... if I had controlled her, Emma would have been destroyed." 
David confessed, his voice choked with emotion. 

"Emma's power was wild and destructive. If I had used my Level 6 strength to suppress her, she would have been crushed by my Pressure. She would have spent her entire life rebelling like a wild animal, or she would have lost her sense of self completely. Just like those kids Director Vance keeps locked in the basement."

David's eyes moved to Emma. 

"When I was an Enforcer, I saw countless kids destroyed in the name of 'containment.' I couldn't let my daughter become one of them. So... I chose to lose my power. I figured if I became weak, at least Emma wouldn't be crushed by my Pressure and lose herself."

Emma's eyes widened in shock. She couldn't believe it. The father she thought avoided her because he didn't love her had actually sacrificed his most precious power just to keep her alive. 

"But David..." 
Sarah sobbed, looking at her husband. 

"You should have at least told me the truth. For the nine years you were helpless, I resented you. Every day in this hell... I blamed you in my head for being an incompetent father."

"I'm sorry I lied to you." 
David took Sarah's hand. 

"But if I had told you the truth, you would have definitely feared Emma while placing all the blame on her. You would have resented her, thinking, *'You stole your father's power.'* I didn't want Emma to be resented by you. I wanted to stop you from looking at her like a monster."

A guttural sob tore from Sarah's throat. 
She dropped to her knees and reached out to Emma. 

"Emma... Emma, sweetie, Mommy's so sorry."

Sarah stroked Emma's cheek. It wasn't the trembling, terrified touch of someone walking on eggshells. It was a rough, unpolished touch, but it held the genuine warmth of a mother. 

"Mommy was scared of you. Every day, I was terrified you would wake up, terrified you would get angry. I shouldn't have been like that. When you broke a window, I should have scolded you. When you cried, I shouldn't have been afraid; I should have hugged you. Mommy was too weak, too much of a coward. I turned you into a monster. Please forgive me, Emma..."

Sarah pulled Emma into a tight embrace. 
Emma bawled in her mother's arms like a little baby. Sam, completely oblivious to what was happening, patted his sister's back. 

Ian's eyes grew red as he watched them. 
His family hadn't used their Pressure to destroy each other. They had just tried to protect each other the wrong way, and the misunderstandings had piled up into a massive, impenetrable wall. Under the curse of Pressure, they were all just clumsy, tragic victims. 

When the crying subsided a little, Emma looked at Ian with bloodshot eyes. 

"Ian."

Her voice was quiet, but clear. 

"I always wanted you to hit me."

Ian flinched in surprise. 

"Every time I smashed something in the house, every time I flipped the dinner table... I waited for you to get mad at me, to yell at me, and maybe smack me on the head."

"You were a Level 8. I was a Null. How was I supposed to hit you?" 
Ian replied with a bitter smile. 

"That's why I was sad." 
Emma wiped her tears. 

"If you got mad at me... it meant you were treating me like a person. It meant you hadn't given up on me. But you always avoided me. Like I was a bomb waiting to go off. So I thought I really had become a monster. Because nobody would even bother fighting me."

A dull pain spread across Ian's chest. 
*Because I didn't fight back, I turned this kid into even more of a monster.*

"Ian, hit me just once."
Emma said suddenly. 

"What?"
"You're a Level 10 now. You're stronger than me. So, for all the bad things I did before... just give me one good flick on the forehead."

Ian stared blankly at Emma before letting out a small chuckle. 
He raised his hand and flicked Emma right on the forehead. 

*Flick!*

It was a sharp, crisp sound. A flick powered entirely by pure physical force, without a single ounce of Pressure behind it. 

"Ouch!"
Emma grabbed her forehead, exaggerating the pain. 
But on her lips was a clear, radiant smile—one Ian hadn't seen in nine years. 

Sarah and David watched them, smiling silently through their tears. 
The heavy, suffocating air that had seemed ready to tear them apart just a moment ago was completely gone. Over the cheap plastic table, having understood each other's burdens and tended to each other's wounds, the warmth of something truly called 'Family' had finally taken root. 

"I don't get it."
Sam tilted his head from Emma's lap. 

"But I hope nobody cries anymore."

At the toddler's innocent words, warm laughter filled the room. It was a perfect sense of liberation. 

*BOOM!!*

Right at that moment. 
The front door was blown entirely off its hinges with a deafening explosion. 

The laughter stopped dead. 
Through the acrid smoke, a heavily armed tactical squad from the Federal Pressure Control Bureau stormed into the living room, clad in black helmets and tactical vests. In their hands were assault rifles equipped with Pressure-nullifying suppressors. 

In the exact moment the family had finally learned to look at each other, 
the federal government had kicked down their door to tear them apart.
