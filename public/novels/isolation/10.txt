# PRESSURE
## Episode 10: The Girl Behind the Wall

---

After sweeping up all the broken glass, Ian put the broom down and stood in front of Emma's bedroom door. 

For sixteen years, this door had been the entrance to hell for Ian. The constant terror of never knowing what kind of monster would burst out from behind it to shatter their daily lives. But right now, with the power of a Level 10, this flimsy hollow-core door was just a fragile barrier he could pulverize with a single finger. 

Ian didn't use his Pressure. Instead, just like a normal older brother, he raised his hand and knocked. 

*Knock, knock.*

There was no answer from inside. But Ian could read the flow of the air; he knew Emma was standing right behind the door, holding her breath. 

"Emma."
Ian called out quietly. 

The air on the other side of the door trembled. 
"Go 'way..."
It was a voice as small as a mosquito. Not the command of a tyrant, but the trembling plea of a terrified little kid. 

"Open the door."
"Don't come in! I'm sorry... I'll clean up all the glass I broke, just please don't punish me..."

That desperate, pathetic excuse made Ian's heart drop. Emma was completely paralyzed by the terror that her brother would use his 'Level 10' power to punish her. The absolute, suffocating fear that Emma had inflicted on her family every time she was in a bad mood for the past nine years—she was now receiving it all back in full. 

Ian slowly turned the doorknob. 
The door clicked open, revealing a twelve-year-old girl huddled in the dark corner of the room, hugging her knees to her chest. 

Ian walked into the room, pulled up her desk chair, and sat down in front of her. Emma kept her head buried in her knees, her shoulders shrinking inward. 

"I'm not here to punish you." 
Ian said. 

Emma shook her head, clearly not believing him. 
"Agent Osborne told me... power is rank. You're stronger than me now, so you're the king..."

Ian frowned at the mention of Agent Osborne's name, but quickly swallowed his anger and asked a question. 

"So? When you were the king, were you ever happy? Even once?"

The question was so sudden, and so incredibly heavy. Emma's huddled body went rigid. 

"The whole family walked on eggshells around you, catered to your every whim, and lived in absolute terror of your mood swings. Wasn't that exactly what you wanted?"

"No..."

"If not that, then what? You're the one who made us all live like that."

"It's not what I wanted!"

Emma's head snapped up as she screamed. Her eyes were brimming with tears she had been suppressing for years. 

"I didn't choose this! I was three years old. I didn't even know what was happening. Dad bought me a toy robot and it was too heavy... I just thought I wished it was lighter... and suddenly Mom's minivan was flying in the air and the driveway was breaking apart."

Emma's voice began to break. The Pressure in the room fluctuated wildly, but Ian didn't flinch. He just sat there quietly. 

"When I cried, the windows shattered. When I laughed, the furniture floated. I just... I just wanted Mom and Dad to say I did a good job, and I wanted to play house with you... but Mom shook every time she looked at me, and you wouldn't even look me in the eye. Dad wouldn't even hug me!"

Fat tears began to roll down Emma's cheeks, dropping onto her knees. 

"When I was four, I was playing catch with Dad and I was so stupid, I couldn't control my Pressure. The baseball exploded and broke Dad's arm. Because of me... because of me, Dad's arm snapped and the bone poked out! By the time I realized what I did, it was too late. After that day, Dad never played with me again. Nobody ever played with me again..."

The demonic philosophy Agent Osborne had planted in Emma's head: *'Fear is just another word for respect.'* 
Emma had been clinging to that lie to survive. The desperate survival instinct of a child who figured that if nobody was going to love her, she would at least make them fear her so they had to look at her. 

"Because you guys looked at me like a monster! I had no choice but to act like a monster! Because I thought if I acted nice, if I showed I was weak, you guys would abandon me!"

Emma slammed her fist against the floor. 
*BANG!*
The cheap motel carpeting tore, and a crater formed in the concrete beneath it. 

She was wailing. She was vomiting up the tears she hadn't been able to shed for nine years, finally ripping off the mask of the tyrant to show her true face. 

"I wanted... I wanted to be normal, too. I just wanted to live without all this Pressure stuff like you... I just wanted to be a normal daughter..."

Emma covered her face with both hands and sobbed hysterically. 
Ian felt like his chest was being ripped open. The realization that the person he had hated the most for sixteen years was actually the loneliest, most terrified little kid in the world crushed him. While the adults had cowardly looked away and avoided her, this child had been walking through hell all by herself for nine years. 

Ian stood up from the chair and approached Emma. 
He dropped to his knees on the floor and carefully reached out to her. 

"Don't touch me!"

Emma flinched violently and scrambled backward. 

"Don't touch me! My Pressure might lash out again and hurt you... I can't control it either..."

"I don't care."

Ian said firmly. He didn't back down. He reached out, grabbed Emma's small, shaking shoulders, and pulled her into his arms. 

"Let go! I might kill you!"
Emma struggled and thrashed in Ian's embrace. The highly unstable Level 8 Pressure waves battered Ian's body mercilessly. 

Ian felt an agonizing pain, like his skin was being sliced open. But he didn't raise his defensive barrier. He took the pain with his bare body, holding Emma tightly. 

"It's okay. It doesn't hurt."
Ian whispered into Emma's ear. 

"It does hurt! Your back is bleeding right now!"
"It doesn't hurt at all. Compared to how much you've been hurting for the last nine years, this is absolutely nothing."

At those words, Emma's thrashing stopped like a lie. 
Her shoulders trembled violently, and then she buried her face in Ian's t-shirt, grabbing fists full of the fabric. 

"Ian..."
"I'm sorry, Emma. I'm so sorry I left you alone."

Emma buried her face deep into Ian's chest and began to cry out loud. 
There was no Pressure in the sound of her crying. No destructive force. It was just the very ordinary, heartbroken sobbing of a lost twelve-year-old girl who had finally found her way home. 

The massive, impenetrable wall of isolation that had divided the Han family for so long was finally, silently, crumbling down.
