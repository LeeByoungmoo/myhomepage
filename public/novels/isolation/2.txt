# PRESSURE
## Episode 2: The Low-Tier Classroom

---

"I heard the Top-Tier bastards turned on the anti-gravity modulators in their cafeteria today. Who do they think they are, astronauts?"

Lunchtime. The dingy corner of the Low-Tier cafeteria. Leo grumbled, viciously mashing a tasteless, mass-produced meatball with his plastic spork. Ian stared silently at his tray. 

The architecture of the school was brutally segregated by Pressure levels. Students from Levels 1 to 3 were crammed into the decaying north annex. The mid-tiers, Levels 4 to 6, occupied the main building. And the Top-Tier—Level 7 and above—enjoyed the state-of-the-art specialized training grounds in the south wing. 

They wore the same uniforms, but they lived in entirely different universes. 

"Hey, Null. You listening to me?"
"Yeah. Astronauts."
"I’m saying, the feds feed those pricks custom nutritional diets just to help them lift a spoon, and we’re stuck chewing on expired Salisbury steak. Does having low Pressure give you a cast-iron stomach or something?"

Ian offered a faint, tired smile at Leo’s bitter joke. Leo, a Level 1, was always complaining. But turning that resentment into sarcasm was the only weapon he had. It was how the weak survived the world. 

"Wait, who's that?"

Leo jutted his chin toward the cafeteria entrance. Ian followed his gaze. 

It was the transfer student from this morning. Sienna, was it? She had a neat bob cut and dead, unreadable eyes. She was holding a tray, scanning for an empty seat. 

What was strange was the 'air' around her. 

Low-Tier kids usually didn't pay much attention to personal space. Because their Pressure was so weak, bumping into each other or standing close didn't cause any physical discomfort. But as Sienna walked, the students around her instinctively parted. It was as if an invisible barrier enveloped her. 

*The density of her Pressure... it's different.*

Ian held his breath. Having survived next to Emma’s high-density Pressure every single day, he had developed an animal-like instinct for detecting microscopic changes in the air. 

The air around Sienna was quiet, but it was compressed. Hard as a diamond. That was absolutely not the Pressure of a Level 1 or 2. She was at least a Level 6. Maybe higher. 

*What the hell is someone like that doing in the Low-Tier annex?*

While Ian was questioning her presence, Sienna drifted toward their table. Specifically, she sat at the empty table right next to theirs. Leo's eyes lit up with curiosity, and he opened his mouth to talk to her, but Ian swiftly kicked him in the shin under the table. Leo clamped his mouth shut. 

"Ow, you crazy bastard."
"Shut up. Just eat your food."

Ian lowered his head. Nothing good ever came from getting tangled up with the strong. That was the only survival rule he had learned in his sixteen years of life. 

***

At that same moment, back in the family home. 

A suffocating silence filled the living room. Sarah, the mother, was locked inside the master bedroom, holding her breath. Sam, the youngest, had finally cried himself to sleep in her arms. 

Two people sat across from each other on the living room sofa. 
Emma, and her 'mentor,' Agent Osborne. 

An agent of the Federal Pressure Control Bureau, and an overwhelming Level 9 user, Agent Osborne visited Emma once a month to 'inspect' her condition. 

"You don't look so good, Emma."

Osborne spoke softly, lifting his coffee mug. But his voice carried an undeniable, crushing weight. 

"How is your training lately? Is controlling your Pressure getting easier?"
"Yes." Emma answered curtly. Her voice was too dry, too hollow for a twelve-year-old. 
"Is your family 'treating' you well?"

Emma’s fingers twitched at the question. A strange smile crept across Osborne’s lips. 

"Remember this, Emma. You are special. You are a child standing at the very pinnacle of human evolution. Your parents and your brother only played the role of breeding and raising you. Fundamentally, you are a different species from them."

"But..."
"But what? Are you sad that they fear you?"

Osborne’s eyes turned ice-cold. In an instant, the air in the living room expanded violently, causing the windows to rattle violently in their frames. The sheer weight of a Level 9 Pressure pressed down on Emma. She gasped for air, her back slamming against the sofa. 

"Fear is just another word for respect. If they don’t fear you, they will try to control you. You weren’t born to be controlled. You were born to rule. Never forget that the only reason your family is still intact is because you 'allow' it to be."

Osborne withdrew his Pressure. The rattling windows stopped, and that sickening silence descended upon the living room once more. 

"Do not become weak. The moment you show weakness, everything you have will turn into a blade aimed at your throat."

Osborne stood up. Even long after the sound of the front door closing echoed through the house, Emma remained curled up on the sofa. 

*Do not become weak.*

She wrapped her arms tightly around her own small shoulders. 
She had lived as a monster for far too long. No one had ever taught her how to be human again.

***

After school. 
Ian was walking down the suburban sidewalk toward his house. As the silhouette of his home came into view in the distance, the memory of his mother's bruised cheek from this morning flashed in his mind. A dull ache throbbed in his chest. 

Then, he heard footsteps behind him. 
An even, steady pace. Light, yet carrying a heavy presence. 

Ian stopped. He turned around. Sienna was standing there. 

"You're a weird one." 

Sienna spoke first. Her tone was flat, devoid of emotion. 

"What do you mean?"
"In the cafeteria earlier. You avoided me. You felt my Pressure, didn't you?"

Ian didn't answer. 

"For a Level 1, your senses are uselessly sharp. Or maybe..." Sienna took a step closer. Her eyes seemed to pierce right through him. "...maybe you're just used to it for some other reason."

"You're imagining things. I'm just tired." 

Ian turned his head and quickened his pace. But Sienna’s parting words struck him squarely in the back. 

"Careful. You have the eyes of someone carrying a bomb. One that's right on the verge of exploding."

Ian faltered for a second, but forced himself to keep walking. The road home felt unusually long today.
