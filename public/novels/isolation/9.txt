## Episode 9: Proof of Blood and Iron

The massive fighting dog’s eyes flashed under the blinding white light, my hunting knife firmly clamped in its jaws.

Thick saliva and the doctor's red blood dripped down its chin, splattering onto the rough stone floor.

"How does it feel? Having your very last variable snatched away."

My husband’s mocking laughter echoed through the speakers.

The beast growled, driven mad by hunger, ready to rip my throat out at any second. If I turned and ran, my back would be shredded before I took a single step.

I took my hand off my bleeding side. Stripping off my thin cardigan, I wrapped it tightly around my left forearm in multiple thick layers.

Grrrrr— Bam!

The massive beast kicked off the stone floor and blotted out my vision. The moment its rotting breath hit my face, I didn't dodge. Instead, I shoved my wrapped left arm deep into its gaping, razor-filled mouth.

Crunch, crack!

The sharp fangs pierced the fabric, shredding the flesh of my forearm and scraping against the bone with a horrific pop.

Pain so intense it whited out my vision slammed into my spine, but just as I calculated, the beast's jaws were now perfectly bound to my left arm.

"Hgggh!"

Swallowing the agony, I reached out with my right hand and grabbed the handle of the knife protruding just slightly past the dog's lips.

Gripping the slippery handle, I violently twisted and yanked. The beast's mouth tore open, and hot blood gushed all over my face.

Without a second of hesitation, I drove the blade straight into the dead center of the beast's thick neck.

The massive, thrashing body stiffened like a lie before collapsing heavily on top of me like a chunk of dead iron.

"Hah... hah..."

Gasping for air, I kicked the dead beast off me and slowly stood up.

Blood was spraying like a fountain from my mangled left arm, but my right hand was once again gripping the knife—the ultimate symbol of control.

Behind the bulletproof glass, the relaxed smile finally vanished from my husband's face.

I flashed a bloody, mocking grin at the glass, then turned and walked toward the darkness behind the heavy iron doors.

But the moment I took my first step into the pitch-black void, something cold and thin caught tightly against my ankle.

Click.

A faint friction sound echoed, and a red digital timer flickered to life in the dark, flashing the numbers '00:59'.

And from a speaker in the empty darkness, a chillingly clear, young boy's voice rang out—not my husband's.

"Mom? Mom, is that you...?"

It was a voice that sent my heart plummeting to the floor.

It was the voice of my biological son. The son I had murdered ten years ago by staging a car accident.
