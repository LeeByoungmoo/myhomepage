# PRESSURE
## Episode 9: The Temptation of Kings

---

Power is like a lethal poison. At first, it’s as sweet as a painkiller numbing a wound, but it gradually takes over every sense in your body until the power itself becomes the only purpose. 

Over the past few days, Ian was slowly getting drunk on that sweet poison. 

Without anyone noticing exactly when it happened, the dynamic in the household had pulled a complete one-eighty. 
The head of the table naturally became Ian's seat. Whenever Ian walked into the living room, Emma, who would be eating at the kitchen island, would quietly lower her head and retreat to her room. That vicious, arrogant twelve-year-old tyrant now shrank at the mere sound of Ian's footsteps. 

His father David's change in attitude was even more dramatic. 
Whenever he addressed Ian, his eyes were filled with a strange awe, bordering on worship. 

"Ian, these are the supplemental funding documents the Bureau sent over. They said you need to sign on the dotted line."

David held the file folder out respectfully with both hands. The dignity of the former Level 6 Enforcer was completely gone. He had perfectly transformed into the loyal errand boy of a Level 10 authority figure. 

Whenever his mother Sarah prepared Ian's meals, she used only the most expensive ingredients. The premium federal rations that were delivered daily were all poured out for Ian. Sarah would stroke Ian's cheek with tears in her eyes. 

"My sweet boy, thank you for growing up so strong. No one will ever look down on our family again."

It looked like the perfect peace Ian had desperately craved for sixteen years. A world without his monster of a sister's rampages, without his father's pathetic lethargy, without his mother's heavy sighs. 

But something was going terribly, terribly wrong. 

***

That afternoon, Ian stopped by the local supermarket to pick up a few things. 
The moment he walked through the sliding automatic doors, the air in the store froze cold. 

People collectively stopped what they were doing and stared at Ian. They recognized him. The 'Level 10 Boy' whose face was plastered across the news every hour of the day. 

As Ian walked down the snack aisle, the shoppers hurriedly parted to either side like Moses parting the Red Sea. Some didn't even dare to breathe loud enough to be heard, lowering their heads to the floor. 

Just then, a middle-aged man pushing a shopping cart rounded the corner and accidentally bumped his shoulder against Ian's. 

"Oh, excu..." 

The man looked up, recognized Ian's face, and went dead pale. The Pressure radiating from the man was about a Level 3. It wasn't a low number for an average civilian, but in front of Ian, he was a speck of dust. 

"I-I'm so sorry! I wasn't looking where I was going..."
The man trembled uncontrollably, bowing at a perfect ninety-degree angle. 

Ian felt annoyed. It was a very petty, selfish kind of annoyance. Just the irritation of having his peaceful grocery run interrupted. 

And the exact moment that split-second of annoyance flickered through Ian's subconscious... 

*CRRRUNCH!*

The steel support beams of the supermarket aisles buckled like taffy. Hundreds of canned goods on the shelves began to rattle and shake violently. Ian's unsuppressed Pressure had involuntarily lashed out. 

"Eek!"

The man shrieked and collapsed to the floor in terror. The surrounding shoppers screamed and ducked behind the display racks, covering their heads. 

Startled, Ian immediately reined in his Pressure. The buckling steel stopped groaning, and the rattling cans fell still. 

Suffocating silence. 
The man sitting on the floor, his face wet with tears, was aggressively rubbing his hands together, begging Ian for mercy. 

"Please, spare me... I swear it was an accident. Please, just forgive me this one time..."

Looking down at that pathetic sight, a shock like an ice pick drove straight through Ian's heart. 

*This scene.*

Exactly ten years ago. 
When Emma was six years old, another kid at the playground had snatched a toy from her. Emma had crushed the kid's parents with her Pressure, and those grown adults had knelt in the dirt in front of a little girl, begging for their lives in exactly the same way. 

And watching that, Ian had cursed Emma with a sickening disgust and hatred. 

*You call yourself family? You're a monster!*

Ian looked down at his own two hands. 
The awe of the public. His mother's blind affection. His father's submission. His sister's terror. 

The true face of this peace wasn't love. 
It was just that the name 'Ian' had replaced the name 'Emma' on the throne he had hated so much. 

*I'm... I'm turning into a monster.*

Ian abandoned his shopping basket and bolted out of the supermarket. He ran toward his house until his lungs burned and he couldn't breathe. 

When he arrived home and stepped into the living room, panting heavily, Emma happened to be in the kitchen getting a glass of water. The second she locked eyes with Ian, she flinched, and the glass slipped from her hand. 

*CRASH!*

Glass shards exploded across the floor. 

"I-I'm sorry..." 
Emma turned pale and immediately dropped to her knees, reaching out to pick up the broken glass with her bare hands. 

Ian's eyes wavered. 
A twelve-year-old girl. The very person who had turned this house into a living hell. The tyrant who had always ruled over them. 
But the person trembling in front of him right now was just a small, pitiful child who had been swallowed whole by the brutal logic of Pressure. 

Ian slowly walked over to Emma. 

"Don't touch it."
Ian said. His voice carried absolutely zero Pressure, zero intimidation. 

Emma flinched and froze. 
Ian crouched down on the floor, grabbed a dustpan and a broom, and started sweeping up the shards. 

"You don't pick up broken glass with your bare hands." 

Emma stared blankly at her older brother's back as he swept the floor. For nine years, in this house ruled by power, she had never seen a sight like this. 

*The strong bending their knees for the weak.*

As Ian emptied the dustpan into the trash, he made a decision. 
He wasn't going to let himself be dragged around by the chains of power anymore. He had to cut off the sweet temptation of the crown and confront the real root cause of everything that had been broken. 

And to do that, he had to listen to the real story of the monster girl hiding behind the wall. 
He had to listen to his sister, Emma.
