# PRESSURE
## Episode 5: Stirring

---

Ian woke up with a migraine. 
It was a pounding pain, like a hammer striking his temples over and over. He tried to sit up, but his body felt incredibly heavy. No, to be precise, the air itself felt heavy. 

*I need to open a window.*

As he twisted his body to get out of the motel bed, the sight before him made him gasp. 
His duffel bag was floating in mid-air. Hovering about a foot off the cheap carpet, the bag vibrated with a faint, low hum. 

Ian rubbed his eyes. He figured he was hallucinating from exhaustion. He reached out to touch the bag, and the moment his fingers grazed it, it dropped to the floor with a loud *thud*. 

"What the... what was that?"

His heart started to race wildly. It was the classic early symptom of Pressure manifestation. The localized zero-gravity effect on surrounding objects. 

*No. That's impossible. I've been a Null for sixteen years. There's no way I'm suddenly manifesting a Level now.*

He shook his head violently, denying it. He brainwashed himself into believing it was just his frayed nerves playing tricks on him from the stress of living in a FEMA motel. 

***

The next morning, the air in the cramped motel room was still as fragile as thin ice. 
Since the 'explosion incident' that day, Emma hadn't taken a single step out of the bathroom, which she had claimed as her own space. The door was locked tight, and not a single sound came from inside. 

Sarah carefully set a paper plate with a pop-tart and a juice box in front of the bathroom door. 

"Emma, sweetie, Mom's leaving your breakfast here. Come out and eat if you get hungry..."

No answer. Sarah stared at the closed door with sorrowful eyes for a long time before turning away. 

On the motel room's tiny round table, the youngest, Sam, was scribbling on a napkin with crayons. Ian walked over to pat Sam’s head before leaving for school. 

"What are you drawing, buddy?"

Sam smiled brightly and held up the napkin. 
Ian’s heart dropped. 

It was a drawing of their family, done in wobbly crayon lines. Dad, Mom, Ian, Emma, and Sam. All five of them were holding hands and smiling brightly. The sun was grinning, and a big heart hovered over the roof of a house they no longer had. 

In the middle of this absolute hell, the perfect family imagined by a four-year-old child who didn't know any better. It was the saddest drawing in the world. 

"Looks great, Sam. I'm heading to school."

Ian fled the motel. It felt like a massive boulder was sitting on his chest. 

***

Even after he arrived at school, the headache didn't subside. 
If anything, it got worse. The air in the classroom felt unusually thick and heavy. The chatter of the students buzzed in his ears like tinnitus. 

"Hey, Null. What's wrong with your face? You look like a zombie that hasn't slept in days."

Leo asked, his eyes filled with concern. 

"Just a headache."
"Wanna go to the nurse's office?"
"No, I'm fine."

Just as Ian put his head on his desk and closed his eyes, someone walked up to his seat. It was Sienna. She stood there, arms crossed, looking down at him with an expressionless face. 

"You smell."
"What?"
"Like burning. The smell of the air scorching around you."

Ian flinched and looked up. Sienna’s eyes didn't seem to be looking at his face, but rather piercing through to whatever was boiling just beneath his skin. 

"What kind of bullshit is that?"
"If you want to hide it, learn how to mask your Pressure. The air around you is so unstable it feels like it's going to explode at any second."

She whispered those words and turned back to her seat. Ian swallowed hard. His fingertips were trembling slightly. 

***

Later that afternoon, a commotion broke out outside the Low-Tier annex. 
The students looking out the windows started buzzing. 

"Hey, look at that! That's a Top-Tier uniform!"

Ian turned his gaze toward the window. A red tie. The mark of the Top-Tier. A massive male student was storming toward the Low-Tier building, huffing with rage. The heat-haze-like distortion of Pressure radiating from him was visible to the naked eye. 

"That's Blake. Level 7. Why the hell is that psycho here?" Leo muttered. 

Blake seemed incredibly pissed off. He marched up to the rusty double doors of the Low-Tier building and slammed his fist into it. 

*CLANG!!*

With a terrifying screech of metal, the steel doors buckled like taffy and blew off their hinges. Screams erupted from the classrooms. 

"Sienna!!! Get your ass out here right now!!!"

Blake's roar echoed across the cracked asphalt of the parking lot. He didn't give a rat's ass about the Low-Tiers. He only had one target: the 'traitor' Sienna, who had fled to the Low-Tier despite possessing a Top-Tier Pressure Level. 

"You think hiding among these powerless pieces of trash is gonna make you one of them?! Come out!"

He stomped his foot. A shockwave of Level 7 Pressure slammed into the entire Low-Tier building. The decaying structure groaned and shook violently. Plaster rained down from the ceiling, and cracks spiderwebbed across the window panes. 

"Aaaaah!"
"Run!"

The Low-Tier students panicked and scrambled out of the classrooms. It was pure chaos. Leo grabbed Ian's arm. 

"Hey, we gotta go! The building's gonna collapse!"

But Ian couldn't move. 
The headache. 
The excruciating, skull-splitting migraine was whiting out his vision. 

And the sounds ringing in his ears. 
*The sound of a building breaking apart. Screams. Faces contorted in absolute terror.*

It overlapped perfectly with the scene from two days ago, when Emma had destroyed their living room. 

A world where the powerless have to hold their breath, tremble, and run for their lives just because someone with power throws a temper tantrum. 
That pathetic, twisted logic of the world finally snapped the last thread of Ian's sanity. 

"Ian! Come on!"

The moment Leo screamed in desperation, a section of the building's exterior wall groaned and collapsed. A slab of concrete the size of a small car plummeted toward the kids running down the hallway—and toward Leo. 

*No.*

Ian reached out his hand. 
And in that exact fraction of a second, the 'thing' that had been crouching dormant inside him for sixteen years broke its chains and awakened. 

*VWOOOOOOOSH—!!!*

A bizarre, absolute silence fell, as if every sound in the world had been muted. 
Followed by a deafening roar, like the fabric of the air itself being ripped apart. 

Blake's Level 7 Pressure, which had been crushing the Low-Tier building, was instantly 'annihilated.' No, it was crushed. 
Crushed by something a hundred, a thousand times more overwhelming and dense. 

The concrete slab froze dead in mid-air, barely three feet above Leo's head. 
Blake fell to his knees, clawing at his throat, completely unable to breathe. 

And in the center of the classroom. 
Ian stood there, blood trickling from his nose. 

Around his bloodshot eyes, the air was warping so violently that the light was bending around him. 
The stirring was over. The monster had opened its eyes.
