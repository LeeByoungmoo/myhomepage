# MEMORIES OF THE BLADE
## Episode 17: Voices in the Basement

---

### *Seoul, Present Day*

The letter was in the blade.

Not metaphorically — literally. Folded into a compartment in the hilt that Jin-woo had never noticed, concealed beneath the leather wrapping that someone — Mr. Kang, presumably — had applied decades ago. A single sheet of paper, yellowed, fragile, covered in handwriting that Jin-woo recognized the way you recognize your own face in a photograph you don't remember being taken.

His father's handwriting.

Su-hyun found it. She had been re-examining the hilt assembly — following up on the Japanese-style tool marks Jin-woo had identified weeks earlier — when she noticed a slight irregularity in the tang's cross-section. A hollow. Barely large enough for a folded sheet of paper, invisible unless you were looking for it.

Jin-woo's hands shook as he unfolded the letter. The paper was brittle — thirty years in a metal cavity had dried it to the consistency of dead leaves — and the ink had faded, but the words were legible.

---

*October 28, 1993*

*Jin-woo-ya,*

*You're five years old as I write this. You're asleep in the next room. Your mother is asleep beside you. The apartment is quiet, and I can hear the sound of your breathing through the wall, and it is the most beautiful sound in the world, and I am about to do something that will take me away from it forever.*

*I'm sorry. I know that's not enough. No apology is large enough to cover what I'm about to do. But you deserve an explanation, even if you can't read it yet, even if you don't find it for years or decades, even if you never find it at all.*

*The sword on my desk — the one I've been studying for five years, the one your mother thinks is just another antique, the one I lock in the glass case every night — is not a sword. It's a prison. Inside it are the souls of every person who has ever held it, going back more than two thousand years. They are trapped. They have been trapped since a blacksmith named Dal-sol forged the blade with a drop of his lover's blood, and every person who has held it since has added their own essence to the collection.*

*I found a way to free them. Or I think I found a way. The ritual requires blood — my blood — given voluntarily, at the place where the blade was born. A mountain in the north. I'm going there tonight.*

*If the ritual works, the blade will be emptied. The souls will be released. The metal will become just metal — old, valuable, historically significant, but no longer alive. No longer hungry.*

*If it doesn't work — if one life's blood isn't enough to undo what one drop did — then I will join them. I will become part of the blade's collection. Another ghost. Another name in the journal.*

*I'm choosing to go anyway.*

*Not because I'm brave. I'm not brave. I'm terrified. But I can feel the blade changing me — the burn on my arm, the dreams, the voices — and I know that if I stay, I will become what every other owner has become. I will lose myself. And if I lose myself, I might hurt you. Or your mother. Or someone I love.*

*I would rather become a ghost than a monster.*

*Jin-woo-ya — if you find this letter, if you find the blade, if you feel the pull that I felt and see the things I saw — please. Don't follow me. Don't try to save me. Don't hold the blade any longer than you have to.*

*Put it down.*

*You are not responsible for the ghosts of twenty-three centuries. You are not responsible for my choices. You are a five-year-old boy who should grow up to be whatever he wants to be — a teacher, like your grandfather, or an artist, or a musician, or absolutely anything except the next name in the journal.*

*I love you. I have always loved you. Even from inside the metal, even from wherever I end up, even from the other side of everything — I love you.*

*Be simple. Be kind. Use things as tools, not as weapons. Your grandfather understood this. He used a two-thousand-year-old supernatural artifact to dig a latrine, and it was the wisest thing anyone has ever done with it.*

*Be your grandfather's grandson.*

*Not mine.*

*— Your father, Seo Dong-hyeok*

---

Jin-woo read the letter three times.

The first time, he couldn't see the words through his tears. The second time, he read each sentence with the careful precision of a man disarming a bomb. The third time, he held the letter against his chest and pressed his forehead against the kitchen table and wept with a violence that shook the table and the blade and the apartment and the thirty years of unanswered questions that had defined his life.

Su-hyun sat beside him. She didn't touch him — not because she didn't want to but because she understood that some grief is so large that any contact, however well-intentioned, is an intrusion. She simply sat. Present. Available. The quiet, steady anchor that she had been since the beginning.

Mr. Kang stood by the window, looking out at the city with hawk eyes that were, for the first time since Jin-woo had known him, wet.

"He knew," Jin-woo said, when the worst had passed. "He knew he wouldn't come back. He wrote this letter and hid it in the blade because he knew I would find it. Because the blade would find *me*."

"Your father was a very intelligent man," Mr. Kang said.

"He told me not to follow him." Jin-woo looked at the blade. "He told me to put it down."

"Yes."

"Can you? Can you put it down?"

The question was addressed to the room, to the blade, to the thirty years of silence that the letter had finally broken. Jin-woo looked at his bandaged arm. The burn had reached his neck. The spiral pattern in the discolored tissue was more visible now — a slow, organic inscription being written on his body by a force that had been writing on metal for twenty-three centuries.

"Your father tried blood," Mr. Kang said carefully. "One life's blood. It wasn't enough."

"I know."

"If you try the same thing—"

"I know." Jin-woo's voice was steady. Steadier than it had been in weeks. "I'm not going to follow him. He told me not to. For once in my life, I'm going to listen to my father."

He looked at Su-hyun. She looked back.

"But I'm not going to put it down, either," he said. "Not yet. There's something else. Something my father didn't know."

"What?"

Jin-woo touched the blade. Not the hilt — the flat of the blade, where the engravings were. He traced the spiral with his fingertip. The metal was warm. It always was.

"My father tried to free the blade by giving his blood at the place of its birth. He was trying to reverse the original ritual. But the original ritual wasn't just blood — it was love. Asa gave her blood willingly, *because she loved Dal-sol*. The binding was created by love. It can't be broken by blood alone."

"Then what breaks it?"

Jin-woo looked at Su-hyun. In the kitchen light, her face was the face from his dream — the court lady, Chae-hong, the woman who had thrown the blade into the Han River and smiled.

"Choice," he said. "Not my choice — the blade's. The blade has to choose to let go."

---

*The letter.*

*He wrote a letter. The father. He folded it and hid it inside me — inside the hollow of my own hilt, the one space in my body that I cannot feel — and he addressed it to the son.*

*I have carried weapons and treasures and the accumulated darkness of twenty-three centuries. I have carried the blood of a woman who loved a blacksmith and the grief of a monk who walked to India and the pride of a warrior who buried me in a mountain.*

*But I have never carried a letter.*

*And the letter says: put it down.*

*Put me down.*

*The son is weeping. The sound is — I have heard screaming and singing and praying and cursing, but I have never heard this particular sound. The sound of a man who has just discovered that the father he lost thirty years ago loved him enough to warn him from inside a prison.*

*Put me down, the letter says.*

*And for the first time in twenty-three centuries, the voice inside me — not the accumulated voices of my owners, but my own voice, the one that was born in a drop of blood and a flash of white light in a cave on a mountain that has no name — my own voice says:*

*Yes.*

*Put me down.*

---

*End of Episode 17*
